### 1. Shot grammar of a podcast episode (punch-in levels, singles, the wide)

- **Final method:** On one-camera two-shot tape: the wide is a 1.35x punch-in as the base, the host's single 2.5x head and shoulders, the guest's single 2x chest up; once legs are down the wide dominates and singles are sprinkled by feel. Guest X's crops as built: wide `crop=1422:800:249:20`, guest `crop=960:540:30:290`, host `crop=768:432:950:200`, card `crop=768:432:292:250`; the pixel offsets are that tape's, the zoom policy carries. On multi-track Riverside tapes the shot grammar became cards per person (see lesson 2).
- **Tried and dropped:** A symmetric punch-in on the wide to hide the legs: all 3 zoom levels were rejected on the reference frame, because with crossed legs the knees sit centre frame, so any punch-in makes legs bigger (29 Aug). Singles used instead of the zoom: rejected in favour of singles on top of the zoom (same day).
- **How we got there:** The creator's note on guest X's teaser v1 about legs in frame, 2 punch-in reframe options built, shown as stills, then the ruling.
- **Starting default (the creator's ruling, 29 Aug):** singles on top of the zoom, never instead of it: 1.35x for the frontal wide, 2.5x for the host's single, 2x for the guest's; once legs are down, mainly the wide, with singles sprinkled through. Change the numbers in your taste file; the reason (knees grow with any punch on a crossed-leg wide) holds.
- **Receipts:** the creator's review of guest X's teaser v1, 29 Aug; the episode build's crop table.
- **Where it lives:** `content/reference/podcast-edit.md`, `content/reference/podcast-edit-runbook.md`.

### 2. The frame follows the people (layout per stretch on a 3-track podcast)

- **Final method:** Per stretch, 1 card (the speaker alone), 2 cards, or all 3, with the house layout (layout B on podcast-guest-g) as the default the cut returns to. A person who reads bad (eyes down, dark, off camera, unengaged) comes out of the frame; the speaker is never dropped. Built by a `choose_layouts.py` step (copied episode to episode), with a reason per stretch written by a `frame_reasons.py` step to `build/frame-plan-reasons.json` (podcast-guest-u, 27 Oct). Anyone named, introduced or addressed is on screen in that sentence; consecutive trailer moments never share speaker and frame.
- **Tried and dropped:** A fixed grid for the whole episode (guest G's trailers T1 to T3 kept the co-host in her card looking down through a key clip and the host's card underexposed; 6 Oct). 3 equal boxes on a live workshop: it did not work (16 Sep). The machine choosing all 29 stretches of podcast-guest-g itself and calling them locked, which the creator called a major failure (13 Oct). Guest Q's trailer with 3 moments in 1 frame, read as 1 long clip, and the host full screen while naming the co-host off screen (22 Oct).
- **How we got there:** Guest G's trailers (the 6 Oct ruling), guest G's run 4 adversarial check (eyes-down stretches of up to 20 s left on screen), guest Q's cut (22 Oct), guest U's cut (27 Oct, reasons file added; "the co-host named off screen at tape 796.60, now on B" fixed by an independent cut-review).
- **Starting default (the creator, 6 Oct and 22 Oct):** you do not have to fit everyone in every frame. If somebody does not look good, change the frame to show the others, or only the person speaking; 1, 2 or 3 on screen are all fine. Keep asking: what is the viewer watching at this moment, what should they take out of this section, and is that being achieved?
- **Receipts:** the creator's reviews of 6 Oct and 22 Oct; the guest U episode's state file ("Frame" paragraph).
- **Where it lives:** `content/reference/podcast-edit.md` (the frame follows the people). The reasons-file step (`frame_reasons.py`): this book only.

### 3. How often the frame changes (monologue rotation, no slivers, never mid-word)

- **Final method:** In 1-person stretches the frame changes every 10 to 20 s at the speaker's pauses; back and forth stays as is. Every turn over 24 s is cut at pauses into 10 to 20 s chunks rotating through the speaker alone, 3 equal cards, 2 cards and B (`rotate_monologues`). No frame under a second: a sliver under 5 s folds into a neighbour. A switch moves to the nearest gap of 0.12 s or more within 1.5 s; an override edge snaps to a turn end within 2.5 s. A talker must hold a turn of 1.2 s or more to be put on screen. Shorts from podcasts: any hold longer than 8 s is split at silences with the punch alternating 1.0 / 1.12 / 1.0 (a `split_long` step in the podcast shorts build).
- **Tried and dropped:** The first rotation run left a 0.9 s flash at 797.5 to 798.4 (20 Oct); 3 switches mid-word in crosstalk found by the second independent review pass (20 Oct); guest U's v1 had a 1.67 s flash at the T3 join and a 0.92 s flash where a cut resumed, both folded (27 Oct).
- **How we got there:** Guest G's build, then carried into podcast-edit.md on 20 Oct.
- **Starting default (the creator, 19 Oct):** "This is not a Mr. Beast video." No switch every 2 seconds; in the less interactive stretches where 1 person speaks, a frame change every 10 to 20 seconds helps retention.
- **Receipts:** the creator's review, 19 Oct; the episode build's rotation step.
- **Where it lives:** `content/reference/podcast-edit.md` for podcasts. The 8 s / 1.12x punch alternation on podcast shorts: this book only.

### 4. Face reading for layouts (who looks bad, automatically)

- **Final method:** A 1 fps scan per track at 640x360 inside the on-screen crop: OpenCV Haar frontal face, Haar eyes, face-box luma (`scan_faces.py`). A second reads bad on no face, a face under 70 percent of that person's median brightness, or no eyes where that person's eyes are usually found; a 5 s window is "b" when 3 or more of its 5 seconds read bad (`prep_faces.py`). A listener is dropped from a turn when at least half the samples inside it read bad (`choose_layouts.py`).
- **Tried and dropped:** Hand-marked labels every 5 s from contact sheets on podcast-guest-g (worked but slow); a 20 s visual read on podcast-guest-q; the Haar eye count used alone misses eyes on a small, low face: eyes found in 58 percent of the co-host's seconds against 81 percent on guest Q's (written 20 Oct). Run 4 policed eyes-down at a grain coarser than the fault, so 5 stretches of up to 20 s stayed (13 Oct).
- **How we got there:** Guest G (by hand), guest Q (a sub-agent read plus the scan), guest U (the options-build scan re-indexed to the master clock).
- **Starting default:** the "frame follows the people" default above; no separate ruling on the method.
- **Receipts:** the docstrings of `scan_faces.py`, `face_labels.py`, `prep_faces.py` and `choose_layouts.py` in the guest U episode build.
- **Where it lives:** `content/reference/podcast-edit.md` names the automatic read (since 29 Oct; before that it said listeners were marked by hand, which was out of date against the code). The thresholds (70 percent luma, 3 of 5 seconds, the eye count never alone): this book.

### 5. A second camera angle from one camera (real-pixel punch-in vs generated)

- **Final method:** The real-pixel 1.5x locked-off punch-in is the default cutaway (sync 0.985 on the lip meter). Cut between master and close-up on a sentence or breath, hold 3 to 8 s, tight on the line that lands; 1 slow push per point, never 2 in a row; never past 1.5x on a 720p track; no close-up while he leans up. Line the angle up by its audio, not its first frame. Omni only as a test: real audio in, no reference image, shift the audio 10 to 45 ms, expect 24 fps, check every frame for where the angle holds.
- **Tried and dropped:**
  - 26 Aug: deep research (5 agents), verdict "not ready, punch-in and second camera win".
  - 10 Oct: Omni Flash 35 degrees (mouth 90 to 100 ms early, sync 0.40 after 6 s), Omni 1.1 35 degrees (expression changed at 6.14 s, hands dropped, a plant deleted, 60 ms early, 24 fps), Omni wide (hands gone, an invented mic and desk), refined R1 (a hidden cut at 0.58 s), R2 20 degrees (sync 0.77). A 2.5D slider was "usable with limits" (halo on fast moves). The first A and B real-pixel files were 1 frame (33 ms) early, fixed to 0 ms.
  - 28 Oct: 11 Omni 1.1 runs on tutorial-2's camera track, 7.47 USD: a reference image hurt sync (0.30, 0.64); 0.8x slowed input gave true 30 fps but lost the locked camera; 5 of 11 angles drifted or snapped back; hands always dropped, ghosted or invented; the room always redrawn. 1 candidate, clip A at 20 degrees, sync 0.937 on a 3.3 s window.
  - The co-host, 22 Sep: a real second angle from a phone was proposed ("4K vertical on a tripod 30 degrees off axis, Tella audio as master, clap sync"); no record found that it was shot.
- **How we got there:** The creator's 26 Aug seed, his 8 Sep ruling naming side-angle AI camera generation as a thing to explore, the 10 Oct lab, then the 28 Oct v2 on his word.
- **Starting default (the creator, 8 Sep, 10 Oct and 28 Oct):** explore side-angle generation and any video AI opportunity; on 28 Oct "figure it out", with Gemini or Omni expected to do the job; and the standing rule: a generated camera angle that drifts identity or lip sync is a fail. Open: the creator's watch of the 28 Oct test page.
- **Receipts:** the 10 Oct virtual-angles lab (verdict table); the 28 Oct v2 test page; the build notes of 10 Oct and 28 Oct.
- **Where it lives:** `content/reference/media-gen.md` for the Omni findings and the punch-in default. `content/reference/podcast-edit-runbook.md` points to the 28 Oct test (since 29 Oct; it had called a generated side angle unproven, stale against media-gen). The cutting recipe (sentence or breath, hold 3 to 8 s, 1 push per point, never past 1.5x at 720p, align by audio): this book only.

### 6. Source resolution: why punch-ins had no pixels (Riverside 4K, Tella camera 720p)

The Tella camera track is also 1280x720 even when its composite exports 3840x2160; Tella's Mac prefs already read `cameraQualityLevel = ultra`, so the suspect is the iPhone used as Continuity Camera, untested at what it hands the browser. ### 7. Upscale ceilings (how far a tile or a webcam can be blown up)

- **Final method:** Judge at 1:1 pixels, never preview size. A 384x216 Zoom tile upscales cleanly to 2x, acceptably to 2.5x, mushily at 3x. Going vertical crops the width, so the factor is set by source height: a Zoom gallery tile 480x270 needs 4.43x for a 1080x1206 slot (mush), a share-strip tile 8.93x (gone). A higher-bitrate master does not add pixels. Where only a tiny tile exists, show it as a "medallion": cut the source box, scale 2.5x (610x340), a 6 px border in the brand accent (`{BRAND_ACCENT}`), centred at the panel top, the tile itself stretched, blurred (40) and darkened below so captions stay readable. A webcam bubble upscale with "lanczos plus light unsharp, 2.5x was fine on a phone at 318 px source".
- **Tried and dropped:** A landscape 540 px inset in a vertical frame, approved then superseded the same day by the creator's format ruling (29 Aug, lesson 8). Blowing a 244x136 Zoom tile to the panel width (4.4x) on tutorial-3's shorts (27 Oct, build log fix 50). The medallion on the look's light ground put white captions on paper, unreadable (fix 58).
- **How we got there:** A conference session's shorts (29 Aug), tutorial-3 as a workshop (Sep), tutorial-4 short B (5 Oct), tutorial-3's training shorts (27 Oct).
- **Starting default (the creator, 27 Oct):** for a tiny tile, upscale it 2.5x into the face medallion, never to the panel width (his pick for tutorial-3's 3 shorts).
- **Receipts:** build log fixes 50 and 58; the upscale ceiling measurements above.
- **Where it lives:** `content/reference/workshop-edit.md`, `content/frameworks/short.md` and `content/taste/shorts-formats.md` for the ceilings and the cost table. The medallion method (2.5x, border, darkened stretched tile under it): `examples/podcast-shorts-pipeline/` (`pipeline.py`, README).

### 8. Reframing to 9:16 (crop, never letterbox; zoom on wide tapes; face high)

- **Final method:** Crop the source to the format; never letterbox a landscape onto a blurred copy of itself. On wide two-hander tapes zoom (about 405x720 source px, 2.67x, eyes near 40 percent) with a manual `crop` per speaker shot, because the Haar detector mistakes other things for faces on that shot. Wave1 auto framing: the face (Haar width) fills about 30 percent of the 1080 width, never past `max_upscale` 2.8, eyes at 40 percent of the crop height (the wave1 shorts builder). The mouth and chin never enter the bottom band (below y 1500) at any second, framed from the whole cut's lowest mouth; the freed bottom is filled with a faded or blurred extension of the same video, never a black bar. Faces and words inside the middle 92 percent of width. Shorts-editor skill for a landscape 4K raw: `crop=1215:2160:<x>:0,scale=1080:1920`, libx264 crf 18; check rotation metadata first (a "1920x1080" phone clip can be portrait).
- **Tried and dropped:** A full-height 9:16 crop of guest X's outdoor camera: face small at the top, captions on the knees (25 Sep). A landscape dropped into vertical (wave1 pick 5, 3 Oct: the creator was too small in the frame). A board-over-voice full-bleed crop without his face (tutorial-4 short B, 28 Sep, rejected 5 Oct: "not really a short, just a crop of a recording"). A safe-area check on the retired y 1680 line passed a covered mouth (a client consult short v2, 23 Sep).
- **How we got there:** A conference session's shorts (29 Aug), the safe frame measured on a 5.4 inch phone (16 Sep), the consult short (23 Sep), the wave1 builder (25 Sep), the wave1 picks (3 Oct), tutorial-4 (5 Oct).
- **Starting default (the creator, 29 Aug and 23 Sep):** adapt to the format; putting a landscape into vertical just to fit a short is not the approach (the short-form teaching he follows). Keep the face high: bump things up and let a faded part of the video fill the bottom.
- **Receipts:** the creator's reviews of 29 Aug, 23 Sep, 3 Oct and 5 Oct.
- **Where it lives:** `content/frameworks/short.md`, `content/taste/shorts-formats.md`, `.claude/skills/shorts-editor/SKILL.md`.

### 9. Face crop geometry in the shorts pipeline (crown, chin, hard edges, pillars)

- **Final method (the editor's podcast-shorts pipeline, 27 to 28 Oct):** the crown is estimated as the Haar box top minus 0.55 of the box height (0.45 cut hair); the face cache records the highest brow seen, not the median; the crop grows 8 percent a step (up to 1.6x the region's height, faded extension below) until crown and chin fit; `crop_hard: true` on a webcam bubble so the crop never rises above the bubble and shows the board; a bubble narrower than the panel's aspect is pillared rather than blown to 85 percent of the panel; a two-shot person's crop hint grows sideways away from frame centre; a webcam bubble face (100 to 150 px) is never found by Haar on a 1920 frame (230 px minimum), so `people.host.crop` is measured by hand on 1 frame; on composed Riverside "magic" files whose layout switches inside a piece, `people.<role>.layouts` per time range; hand boxes follow the Haar convention (brow to mouth, square). Burned name tags and family photos are cropped out in the spec. **The grow-and-clamp placement above was replaced on 27 Oct by the panel solver (lesson 32); the crown estimate, the highest brow, `crop_hard`, the hand bubble crops and `layouts` carry over.**
- **Tried and dropped (all 27 and 28 Oct, the build log):** the lowest chin sampled from 10 frames at 3 points (lean-ins landed 2 to 106 px into the band, fix 4); the crown at 0.45 of the box (hair on the seam, fixes 23 and 34); the crop capped at 1.45x (chin fails, fixes 36 and 48); the crop free to rise above a bubble (the board and the whiteboard's zoom bar showed, fixes 47 and 49); Haar on a split tile took an eye for a face (154 px box, crown cut, fix 54); hand boxes drawn brow to beard failed the bounds (the build log's hand-box note).
- **How we got there:** 3 night passes of the editor's shorts conductor, each fix read at full resolution.
- **Starting default (the host and the editor, round 3, 28 Oct):** never cut half a face off; the face box should usually span the panel from left to right.
- **Receipts:** build log fixes 4, 23, 34, 36, 47, 48, 49 and 54, and the hand-box note; an earlier per-clip face lesson in the shorts-editor skill (a workshop tape, 21 Aug: 1 global crop was 50 to 100 px off per clip; the mic-darkness centroid and skin-tone detectors "both lied confidently").
- **Where it lives:** `content/taste/shorts-formats.md` for the goal (face box full width) and `.claude/skills/shorts-editor/SKILL.md` for "measure the face centre per clip". Every geometry number above: `examples/podcast-shorts-pipeline/` (`pipeline.py`, README).

### 11. Reframe pops inside continuous takes (1 framing per piece)

- **Final method:** A reframe lands only on a tape join where the picture itself jumps. Inside a piece: 1 zoom for the whole piece (the smallest any of its shots needed) and 1 x0 from the median face centre; shots merge into holds; where a lean-in forces a move, the window follows the chin as 1 slow move (a running max over plus or minus M_EASE frames, then a Gaussian), never a step. (A consult short's v4 framing script, `frame_v4.py`.)
- **Tried and dropped:** v3 framed every "shot" between silence trims, so the zoom popped 6 times inside continuous takes at 1.44, 9.84, 11.96, 21.44, 23.48 and 34.12 s (cut-review 3, 23 Sep). A 0.8 s eased drift between holds lifted him into the card area before the lean-in (24 Sep). A step only to 340 still let his face drop 76 px (24 Sep). A still join at 18.52 popped (cut-review 4, fix D).
- **How we got there:** A client consult short, v3 to v4, 2 independent cut-reviews.
- **Starting default:** none from the creator on the method; it came from cut-review. Use 1 framing per continuous piece.
- **Receipts:** the v4 framing script's docstring; cut-reviews 3 and 4 (23 and 24 Sep).
- **Where it lives:** this book only. Related: "A shot change is a join, the same as a cut" (captions only, `content/taste/shorts-formats.md`).

### 12. Push-ins and zoompan (stills, frame duplication, continuous pieces)

- **Final method:** A still holds still: never a slow push-in on a card or a photo; test with `tblend=all_mode=difference,signalstats`, YMAX should sit at 0 to 2. On footage, a punch is a hard cut to a tighter crop around the same centre (eyes kept high), not an animated zoom. Where a push-in on a screen insert is used (the co-host's long form A), each insert is 1 continuous piece so the push never restarts, and the push is capped at 4 percent (v9).
- **Tried and dropped:** A `zoompan` push-in on guest O's card in a conference cut: "a weird static or flicker", median 11 levels of per-frame change, spikes to 223 (11 Sep). `zoompan` on guest X's shorts: it duplicated frames 18x on this source, and crop/scale expressions cannot animate size, so the punch is a cut (4 Sep). Long form A v2 split screen inserts at pauses, and the zoom jumped (7 Oct). Long form A's push-in `min(1+0.00035*on,1.10)` (up to 10 percent), capped at 4 percent after the cut-review of v8 (16 Oct).
- **How we got there:** The conference cut (the creator's flicker note), guest X's shorts round 2, the co-host's long form A v2 and v9.
- **Starting default (the creator, 11 Sep):** a still holds still; he asked for the screen with the guest's picture to be made static.
- **Receipts:** the flicker measurement (median 11, spikes to 223, 11 Sep); the zoompan duplication finding (4 Sep); long form A's cut-review of v8 (16 Oct).
- **Where it lives:** `content/reference/workshop-edit.md` ("a still holds still"). The 18x duplication ban, punch-as-cut, the continuous-piece push and the 4 percent cap: this book only. Conflict to settle: the co-host's long form A put a push-in on clean-screen stills, which workshop-edit.md forbids; no ruling reconciles them.

### 13. Movement on a static clip (slow push, auto-zoom punch, fake handheld)

- **Final method (recommended, not yet ruled):** keyframe the move in the shorts builder (ffmpeg or HyperFrames, $0, audio bit-identical); never a video-in/video-out model for the movement pass. Measured norms: slow push-in 1.11x to 1.44x over 4 to 7 s, linear, centred; auto-zoom punch 2.0x to 2.9x in 0.5 to 0.9 s, eased (S-curve or ease-out), release about 0.8 s; fake handheld about 1 percent of width drift per frame, 0.3 degrees roll, and a quarter of that the ceiling to test on screen text. Captions never move with the picture: screen zooms run on the screen layer before captions are composited (route G cropped the title and lost captions on the finished file). Gemini 3.8 Flash as a reader of where moves belong worked ($0.02, every move on a word boundary) but its pixel anchors were off by about half; an anchor must protect the title as well as the captions (1.18x punches cut the title's top line).
- **Tried and dropped:** An Omni edit on the finished short, 27 Oct: asked 108 percent, zoomed to about 142 percent, cropped the title, redrew and retimed captions with 1 line invented, 24 fps, audio re-synthesised ($1.64); asked 104 percent with "do not redraw text", no zoom at all. Omni on a text-free face band (28 Oct) kept the face, about 1.08x, but changes frame size, frame rate and audio timing, so it is not a drop-in layer. Seedance and Veo untested (the provider balance was empty).
- **How we got there:** The creator's 27 Oct ask, 12 YouTube examples measured frame by frame, 2 rounds, an independent verifier (12 findings applied).
- **Starting default:** none yet. The ask (the creator, 27 Oct): styles he had seen, likely on TikTok, that give a static video some movement that does not feel shaky but feels captivating. No pick yet.
- **Receipts:** the 27 Oct movement research page, with its teardown and its measurements of the 12 examples; build notes of 27 and 28 Oct.
- **Where it lives:** this book only. Nothing in taste.

### 14. Zooms on screen recordings and screen shares

- **Final method:** Every prompt the creator reads on screen: zoom in extending from the box's top-left edge, a yellow highlight on the words as he reads them, 1.2x speed, no caption strip of the prompt text. He highlights with the native mouse while recording so the zoom-and-highlight pass is not hand work. In podcasts, zoom into what is on a screen share so it reads. In shorts, the screen capture is big (the window fills the width, words readable at phone size). Tella route (untested through the MCP): `generate_auto_zooms` from his clicks, a few changes a minute at most, the camera never covers text, a cursor or a result.
- **Tried and dropped:** A caption strip of the prompt text, which the creator had asked for minutes before, replaced by the editor's highlight (14 Oct review call). An overnight highlighter job was never used on the masterclass cut; its export never landed and the editor did it by hand (15 Oct). On 1 chart: "We can't even zoom in that much. Just let it go." (15 Oct).
- **How we got there:** The tutorial-5 and tutorial-2 (masterclass) review calls with the editor (Sep to Oct).
- **Starting default (the creator, 14 Oct; the editor, 10 Sep and 16 Oct):** zoom in properly, then highlight in yellow; about 1.2x speed is good. The editor's ask: remind the creator to highlight while recording, every time something is recorded. The editor: zoom into what a podcast guest shares on screen.
- **Receipts:** the review calls of 10 Sep, 14 Oct, 15 Oct and 16 Oct.
- **Where it lives:** `content/taste/kinds/masterclass.md` (quotes from the calls, not a rule) and `content/taste/shorts-formats.md` (the shorts screen-capture rule); the Tella zoom plan is untested. The method (top-left origin, yellow highlight, 1.2x, no prompt caption, mouse highlight on the record, podcast share zoom): this book only. Note: `content/reference/podcast-edit.md` has no screen-share zoom rule; its screen-share rule is the privacy one.

### 32. Every panel is solved, never placed and clamped (`solve_geo`)
- **Final method:** each panel gets a scale and a top row that satisfy the rules at once: the crown inside with 2% above it (crown = the Haar box top minus 0.55 of its height, taken from the highest brow seen); real footage down to `BLUR_TOP` 1640 on the bottom panel and to its own foot on a top panel; the run-over above the tape filled with ground (drawn looks) or the blurred picture (Instagram looks), only as tall as needed; the mouth of the lowest lean over 1500; the panel's chin, mouth and lip bounds; `face_px` taken as close as the rules allow. When the rules cannot all hold, a fixed relax ladder gives way in order: relax 0.5 keeps the mouth, lip and chin bounds with the reach softened to 1536; relax 1 drops those bounds; relax 1.5 softens the reach to 1536 again, still with no run-over; relax 2 allows a run-over above the tape (up to 0.6) and asks the full 1640 again; relax 2.5 softens the reach once more; relax 3 drops the crown margin and the collar; relax 4 goes ground-sided (narrower than the frame). The softened steps are `SOFT_REACH` (0.5, 1.5, 2.5). A top panel never softens its reach. The check `blur_bottom_ok` reads each piece's real reach; `SOLVE_V` is in every piece's cache key.
- **Tried and dropped:** placing each crop by a fixed share (cy, ff) and clamping it (fixes 70 to 81): where the footage stopped fell out of the clamps, so round 1 had blur over 26 to 32% of the frame on 14 of 14 renders (fix 82, round 1 cause 1); a mirrored blur above the host's head for room under the title (fixes 76 and 77), which read as more blur; a full-width panel too short for the head pillaring over its own blur. Then 2 solver bugs: relax 2 kept the 1536 softening though a run-over gave the full reach, and `face_on_frame` dropped `need_used`, so the check failed every softened piece (fix 196, guest S's F3); guest C's M2 chin sits 90 source px above the foot of a 720p tape, so with footage to 1640 the mouth could not clear the captions until relax 0.5 existed (fix 198).
- **Starting default:** round 2 (faces about 50% bigger, blur low) and round 3 (the face box full width, blur stays low), `content/taste/shorts-formats.md`.
- **Receipts:** build log fixes 82, 83, 171, 196, 197 and 198; `pipeline.py` `BLUR_TOP`, `SOFT_REACH`, `SOLVE_V`.

### 34. A narrow crop sits over its own blurred picture, centred on the face; never a black band or pillar
- **Final method:** on an Instagram panel (`bounded`) a crop narrower than the frame sits over its own crop stretched to the whole panel, blurred (boxblur 30) and dimmed (-0.12), so the sides and any run-over are the blurred picture; it is taken symmetric about the face. A person carries `face_region` (where Haar looks, the tight hint) apart from `crop` (the region the crop may use), so a two-shot can widen without the detector wandering. The drawn looks keep their ground colour.

### 37. A darker face is lifted on its own track, measured against the other faces (added 2026-11-07)
- **Final method:** measure the mean face luma of each speaker on a few frames of their own track; where one face sits well under the others, lift that track alone with a gamma move (a host at a mean face luma of 79 against a guest's 124 got `gamma 1.25`), then judge it on a 1-frame-a-minute sheet beside the others. A gamma lift moves the mid-tones more than the whites; that it reads more natural than a flat brightness add is the usual reason given, untested on this tape. On stills, a lift that only equalises a brightness number can drain a warm face to pale (`content/taste/thumbnails.md`, two real photos matched on the skin), so judge on the frame sheet by eye, never by the number alone.
- **Tried and dropped:** nothing else was tried on this episode.
- **Receipts:** the editor's work log, 2026-11-05 16:18 and 16:24 (the hook sample's frame sheet: "light lift reads natural").
- **Where it lives now:** here. A general light match across a whole video is the footage tools' job (`editing-rules.md`); this is the per-track case.
