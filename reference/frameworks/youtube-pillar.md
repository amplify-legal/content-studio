# Framework, the pillar video

> The structural rules for the long-form video a week's other pieces come out
> of, solo or with a guest. The taste files say how you sound. This file says
> how a pillar is shaped. Installed at `content/frameworks/youtube-pillar.md`;
> read by `content`, `script-writer` and `content-waterfall`, alongside
> `content/taste/must-dos.md`, which is the standing list of what every
> recording carries.

---

## What qualifies as a pillar

A 3 question gate, answered out loud before anything gets briefed. Any no kills
it as a pillar. It can still be a short or a post.

1. **Has a real buyer actually said or asked this?** Name the person. Not a
   persona, a person.
2. **Does it move a number they can see?** Time, money, headcount, risk,
   something with a unit on it.
3. **Would you send this to one named prospect with "thought of you"?** If not,
   it is a topic, not a pillar.

No news long-forms. Something worth a same-day reaction gets a short that week
instead.

## Shape

- **Length:** 8 to 14 minutes normally. A definitive end-to-end resource sits
  in a band of about 45 to 90 minutes. Never multi-hour. Inside that band,
  compression is measurable as **chapter density**: the long videos that hold
  put a new concrete thing on screen every 90 to 120 seconds, and the ones that
  die run a beat every several minutes.
- **Packaging before script.** The package passes its 4 questions
  (`content/taste/packaging.md`) before the script is written or the shoot is
  booked.
- **The hook is a standalone 30 to 40 second unit.** Open on a problem, a wrong
  framing, or an earned credential. Turn it. Promise the payoff. Only then say
  who you are. The idea is the hook, never the resume. Keep whatever your own
  handoff line is into the body, the one you say the same way every time. It is
  a real seam and viewers learn it.
- **Section beats:** one idea per section, each built so it could stand alone
  as a short. A workable 6 beat skeleton per section: setup, structure,
  analogy, framework, payoff, transition. Tutorials show the viewer where they
  are: numbered steps and visible progression markers.
- **Key lines are co-created in the interview, never planted by the machine.**
  A line written for somebody to say on camera, in the hope it will clip well,
  almost never survives contact with an actual recording. Get the lines they
  want verbatim in their own words at brief time, mark which beat of each
  section is expected to carry the short, then pull the clip candidates from
  the tape afterwards. Re-recording a line standalone after the long-form is a
  legitimate path and a better one than planting it.
- **Every claim is verified before it is said on camera.** A number, a
  comparison, a claim about somebody else's product. Every figure comes from
  `content/taste/stories.md`. Customer facts stay private, and customers are
  described by shape, never named.
- **The intro beats** (output first, a promise with a finish line, name and
  authority in one breath, a retention line before the first long setup) are
  in `content/taste/must-dos.md`, section B.

## A contrarian belief needs its opponent named

This is the one in here people flatten, and flattening it is what turns a sharp
video into a bland one.

**Say which gospel is being contradicted, and whose it is.** Not "most people
get this wrong". Which people, and what exactly do they say.

The shape that works is: **agrees with the audience, contradicts the industry.**
The viewer should feel defended, and somebody with authority should be on the
other side of the argument. That is what makes a belief worth 12 minutes.

Never collapse that into "agreeable". A video that agrees with the audience and
contradicts nobody has no opponent, and a belief with no opponent is just
pleasant. If you cannot name who disagrees, you do not have the video yet. Go
and find the opponent, or pick another idea.

Naming the opponent has a cost and the cost is the point: somebody will
disagree in public. A belief nobody can disagree with was not worth stating.

## Call to action discipline

One per video, never stacked.

**Ask where it goes at brief time rather than deciding for them.** A mark
around 35% of the way in is a reasonable default to offer, and their answer
outranks it every time, including "at the end, at least not this time". Offer
the default, take the answer, write down the hedge if they gave one.

Which call to action follows the week's mix, roughly: 60% pure value with no
ask, 20% pointing at something free, 20% pointing at the thing you sell.

Watch time is the real ask. No subscribe interruptions: the subscribe ask and
the next video live on the end screen.

**Two asks are not calls to action in this sense, and every video carries
them:**

- **The comment ask** in the last 20 seconds and in the pinned comment: what
  the viewer wants next, or what they did not understand
  (`content/taste/comments-and-pinned-comment.md`). A video with thousands of
  views and zero comments usually never asked.
- **A mid-video connect ask, about 30% in**, offering help with the thing the
  viewer is about to get stuck on. A guideline, not yet a rule: whether it
  replaces the single call to action or sits beside it is yours to decide, and
  the answer goes in `content/taste/must-dos.md`.

**Every promise made on camera ships as a live link in the description**,
fetched and returning 200 in the launch session. A promised resource that is
missing reads as clickbait, and a viewer who feels baited cancels.

## The 2 gates

Every pillar passes 2 reviews, with a mechanical checklist run before each so
the human time goes to taste and not to spotting a missing caption.

1. **Cut review**, after the initial cut, while changing the vision is still
   cheap. Check the cut against the brief's vision section, not against
   somebody's memory of the meeting.
2. **Launch readiness**, at claimed-finished. Approve or bounce. Packaging
   final.

Before the set comes down after the take, ask "what did we miss"
(`content/taste/must-dos.md`, section D). A pickup caught then costs 15
seconds. Caught at the cut review, it costs the set.

The operational bar: fully edited, packaged and ready **4 days before launch
day.** Not the night before. The 4 days are what make the difference between
shipping a video and shipping whatever was rendered by 11pm.

## Guest episodes

Same gate, same key-lines rule, same 2 reviews. What changes is that every
guest is 4 things at once: a lead, a referrer, a possible case study, and a
channel to their own audience.

### Ask every guest before the record

3 questions, asked in the booking thread or the pre-call, never left to the
day. 2 of them change what goes on screen, so they have to be answered before
the edit rather than after.

1. **What distribution do they have?** A list, a group, a community, a
   newsletter, anything with an owned audience. A guest with a list is a launch
   partner. A guest without one is a guest. This is what turns an episode into
   reach instead of a favour.
2. **What is your call to action into their audience?** Pick one. One ask,
   chosen per guest, following the same one-per-video discipline above.
3. **What call to action do they want?** What you put on screen and say out
   loud for them. Ask them to name it rather than guessing, and record the
   exact handle or URL so the lower third is right the first time.

Answers go into the brief in their words. How the run sheet itself is built
lives in `content/taste/podcast-hosting.md`. An unanswered one is written as
`OPEN:` with the question still attached, never as a guess.

## Retro

New structural rules enter from real cut feedback, dated, with the video that
taught them. A rule with no video attached to it is a preference somebody had
once.
