# frameworks

7 frameworks: 6 for the kinds of thing you make, plus 1 for carrying proof.
Installed at `content/frameworks/`. Taste (how you sound, what you refuse)
lives in `content/taste/`; these say how a piece is shaped. Each one says what qualifies,
what shape it takes, and what has to be true before it ships.

**These are starting points, not laws.** They came out of one business making
content weekly, and about a third of what is in them is a ruling somebody made
after getting it wrong in public. Keep the parts that match how you work,
rewrite the parts that do not, and delete anything you will never do. A
framework you edited in week 1 is worth more than one you left pristine and
never opened again.

The one thing worth keeping in all of them is the **retro** section at the
bottom. That is the loop that turns a document into a system: a reason for a
rejection that shows up twice becomes a rule, dated, with the piece that taught
it attached.

## The 3 rules that show up in more than one of these

They are repeated on purpose. If you only take 3 things out of this folder,
take these.

**1. Mined language, never invented language.** Every one of these frameworks
sources its words from something a person actually said out loud, on a call, on
a recording, in a workshop. Transcribe rather than generate. The difference is
audible and it is the whole reason any of this sounds like a person.

**2. A person picks, the machine assembles.** Which clip, which moment, which
angle, which of 2 options. Every framework here puts the taste call on a human
and the labour on the machine. When you find yourself automating a pick, you
have crossed the line this pack exists to hold.

**3. Nothing ships itself.** Drafts, queues and boards are the output
everywhere. A person presses the button, every time.
