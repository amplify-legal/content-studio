## Appendix A, known format types

Reference, not a required field. Cross-check your Section 01 shape sentence
against these. If it matches one, that pattern is your closest guide. If it
does not match, that is fine, write the shape in your own words and the brief
adapts.

| Format type | What it is | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **tutorial-metaphor** | Walks through a topic via one sustained mental model, with a cohesive illustration set | The topic has clean structural pieces that benefit from a metaphor. Beginner to intermediate audience |
| **numbered-list** | The title declares a number, the body delivers exactly that count, 45 to 90 seconds per item, each item one cause and effect | The topic is a set of discrete tactical moves. The number is the contract and the contract is the dopamine. Anti-theory, anti-feature-tour |
| **time-deal list** | "Give me X minutes and I will..." plus a numbered body. Time becomes a second contract alongside the count | When the outcome is the draw rather than the count |
| **comparison decision tree** | "When to use X versus Y versus Z". The body is a set of rules or scenarios, not a sequential teach | The viewer already knows the options exist and is stuck choosing |
| **narrative** | "I spent N hours doing X, here is what I learned". 5 to 8 hard-won lessons with stories, not a tutorial. Talking head plus screenshots as evidence | The audience already trusts you. The lessons are not transferable as instructions, they need the story to land |
| **other** | Novel or hybrid. Write it in your own words | When none of the above fit. Do not force it |

**How to use this table:** read your shape sentence, find the closest row, then
go and find a real video in that shape and study it. Borrow its hook structure,
its section pacing and the kind of visual evidence it uses. Skip the table
entirely if your shape is novel. The brief works without a format tag.
