# Lens: study

Mine the substance out. Use this for a competitor's teaching video, a conference
talk, a podcast episode with a guest worth knowing, a demo of a tool you might
buy, a webinar somebody in your market ran.

The question is not "was this good". It is "what do we now know that we did not
know before, and what of it can we use".

Output this:

## <title>, <creator>, MM:SS

**What it is:** one line. Format, audience, and what the speaker is selling if
they are selling something.

### The argument

Their case in their own structure, 3 to 6 beats, with timestamps. Steelman it.
Do not smuggle in a rebuttal here.

### Claims worth checking

| Time | Claim | Kind | Stands up? |
|---|---|---|---|

Kind is one of: number, mechanism, anecdote, assertion. "Stands up?" is your read
against what you already know, marked **unverified** where you have no basis.
Never mark something verified because it sounded confident.

### Frameworks and language

Anything named, numbered or repeated: a framework, a phrase, a metric, a way of
cutting the problem. Verbatim. This is the part that transfers.

### What is on screen

Slides, demos, documents, dashboards, numbers. Read them off the frames. If there
is a screen showing real data, describe what it actually says.

### For us

3 sections, each allowed to be empty:

- **Use**, what goes into the offer, a script, a report, or the outreach.
- **Watch**, what this tells us about a competitor or the market.
- **Ignore**, what looked useful and is not, with the reason.

### Who is in this

Anyone named worth a contact file or a look. Name, role, why. Do not create the
file from here; hand it to whatever owns the relationship record.

---

Rules for this lens:

- Attribute every claim to a timestamp. A claim with no timestamp does not exist.
- Numbers as numerals.
- Never present the speaker's number as ours. Theirs is theirs until we check it.
- If the video is 45 minutes of nothing, the honest output is short. Write it
  short.
