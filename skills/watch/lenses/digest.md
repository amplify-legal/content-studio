# Lens: digest

The default. Somebody wants to know what is in a video without giving it their
20 minutes.

Output this:

## <title>, <creator>, MM:SS

**In one line:** what the video is and who it is for.

**The claim:** the single thing it argues, in the author's own frame, not yours.

### How it goes

A timeline of the real sections, with timestamps. 6 to 10 rows for a long video,
3 or 4 for a short one. One line each. Not a paraphrase of every paragraph.

### Worth quoting

2 to 5 lines, verbatim, with timestamps. Pick the ones that carry the argument or
that the person reading would actually repeat, not the ones that sound clever.

### On screen

What the frames added that the words did not: a demo, a number, a document, a
chart, a face doing something, a cut that changes the meaning. If the frames
added nothing, say that. It is a finding about the video.

### Worth the time?

One honest sentence. Which part, at which timestamp, or none of it.

---

Rules for this lens:

- **Never paste the transcript into chat.** Synthesize. Offer the raw file if
  they ask for it; the path is printed in the run header.
- When there was no transcript on any route, say so up front. Frames-only is a
  legitimate answer, but the reader has to know that is what they are getting.
- When frames and words disagree, trust the frames and name the disagreement.
- Numbers as numerals, always. Write 36, never the spelled-out form.
