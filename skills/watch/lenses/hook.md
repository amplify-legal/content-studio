# Lens: hook

The first 15 seconds, frame by frame, with one question behind it: what is the
reusable formula here, and could we run it on Tuesday.

Run the script with `--hook` so the opening is pinned at 2 fps. Without that flag
scene detection will hand you 3 frames for the whole open and you will be
guessing.

Output this, and nothing before it:

## Hook, <creator> - "<title>"

**The formula in one line:** `[visual setup] + [verbal claim] + [pattern interrupt]`.

### Frame by frame

| Time | On screen | Said | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|---|

One row per real change, not one row per frame. If nothing changed between 0:04
and 0:09, that is itself the finding and it gets one row saying so.

### The verbal hook, verbatim

Quote the opening word for word, up to the first pattern interrupt or scene
change. Never paraphrase this part.

### The visual hook

- **Frame at 0:00**, describe the literal frame. Shot size, what is in focus,
  where the eyes go first.
- **First cut, zoom or move**, the timestamp, and what it did.
- **Text on screen**, verbatim, with timestamps.

### The pattern interrupt

The one moment in the window where the viewer's brain is forced to re-engage.
Timestamp it and name what changed: audio, cut, claim, or visual.

### The promise

What is the viewer being told they get if they stay. One sentence.

### Steal this

A blank-fill version to fill in for your own video. Specific enough to be useful,
generic enough to reuse.

---

Rules for this lens:

- Timestamps come from the transcript or the frames. Never invent one.
- When a frame and the transcript disagree, **trust the frame** and say so.
- No "great hook!". No advice nobody asked for.
- If the open is weak, say it is weak and name the second where it loses you.
