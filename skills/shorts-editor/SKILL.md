# Shorts Editor

**Load before any step:** `content/PRINCIPLES.md` (the constitution: this system amplifies the creator's judgment, it never replaces it. When it conflicts with a step here, it wins). Then `content/taste/editing-rules.md`, `content/taste/editing-checklist.md`, `content/taste/clip-bar.md` and `content/taste/shorts-formats.md`. Brand values come from `content/brand.md`.

**Safe frame (measured on a real phone, read `content/taste/shorts-formats.md` before any vertical cut):** captions end at or above 78% of the frame height (y=1500 on 1920), nothing that matters sits in the top 12% or the bottom 22%, and the right 16% of the width is dead from 50% down (the action rail). An older rule kept captions 78px from the bottom; it is retired, because every platform covers that band. If your taste file names a dedicated builder for a format, that builder wins over the steps below for that format.

## The law: the transcript is a search index, never evidence

A transcript tells you roughly what words exist and roughly where. It does not tell you **who said them, when the voice actually starts, where the silence is, or what is on screen.** Those 4 are settled by `silencedetect`, by RMS windows over the waveform, or by extracted frames that a person or a script LOOKS at. Never by a word timing.

4 real incidents, each one shipped or nearly shipped:

- **The misattributed line.** A line spoken by someone else on the tape read in the transcript exactly like the on-camera speaker saying it, and was nearly cut as his own self-description. Mouth-motion measurement: **2.1 on that line against 13-22 where he genuinely speaks.**
- **The hairflip at the head.** The transcriber timed the first word to `0.00`; `silencedetect` put the real voice onset at **0.754s**. The transcript said the head was tight, so the clip opened on a hairflip. A person caught it, the transcript never could.
- **3.4s of dead air inside one "word".** The transcriber collapsed a long pause into a single 4.4-second stretched word, so no gap existed for the silence rule to find. The pause survived into a staged cut.
- **4 clipped words and an audible fragment of a removed clause, in one 17-second cut.** Local whisper's boundaries drifted up to **0.5s** across a single passage: it put the onset of the opening word 20ms late, ended one word 60ms early, put another a **full half-second** before its real position, and ended a fourth 330ms early. Cutting to those numbers clipped the opening plosive and 3 word releases. Worse, the splice point taken from the transcript left **130ms of the very clause the edit existed to remove** audible at the join. What settled it was 10ms RMS windows over the waveform, which cost one pass and no model call.
  **The technique, for when `silencedetect` returns nothing** (it did here: 24kHz mono conference audio with a high noise floor): dump the seam window to 16k mono wav and print per-10ms RMS. Voice onsets jump from 1-4 to 200+ in a single window and are unmissable. Run it on **every** in-point, out-point and splice, not only the ones that look risky.

The rule that follows: **before the EDL is written**, run `silencedetect` on the source, and run `verify_speaker.py` on any line being attributed to the speaker (step 3.5). After the render, run `check_safe_area.py` (step 13). None of these is optional because the transcript "looks fine". That is precisely the state all 4 incidents were in.

Turn ONE picked clip into a finished 9:16 short: the speaker in the bottom half, animated brand-accent graphics in the top half, karaoke captions, music. Lineage: adapted from an earlier open reel editor, then rebuilt and extended over dozens of real shorts. The pipeline, the scripts and the **ERRORS NOT TO REPEAT** table are the value. Every row in that table is a bug that already cost someone a re-render.

## Input contract (taste is never automated)

The skill consumes clips **a human already chose**. 2 valid inputs, nothing else:

1. **Planted-zinger timestamps** from the video's brief (the zinger table, if your briefs carry one).
2. **The creator's clip picks** from a clip-selection sitting (timestamps plus which video).

If neither exists, stop and ask for picks. The skill never scans a video for "best moments": that judgment is the point of having a person in the loop, and no auto-clipper replaces it. What a short must be (a transformation, not a reframe; the hook stands alone; the first frame is the promise) lives in `content/frameworks/short.md` and `content/taste/clip-bar.md`. Read both before authoring beats.

## Stack

- **Transcription, 3 engines in a fixed order, and the one that ran is named in the output file's own `_engine` header, never inferred:**
  1. `scripts/transcribe.py` (ElevenLabs Scribe `scribe_v2`, word-level timestamps, `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`) is **the route**, always with `--keyterms-file build/keyterms.txt`. On a real tape both whisper models dropped a whole phrase that Scribe caught, and whisper once rendered the host's own name as two different words in a shipped caption.
  2. `scripts/transcribe_deepgram.py` (Deepgram `nova-3`, same output shape, same `--keyterms-file`, `DEEPGRAM_API_KEY`) is **the declared fallback**, for when Scribe is out of credits, keyless or down. 3 things to know: **cut on its word STARTS, not its ends** (its raw ends run into the next word's start, so the script snaps them to measured energy by default, which removes a 45ms late bias; `--raw-ends` turns that off); **it writes numbers as words** ("twenty seven" where Scribe writes "27"), so captions built on it need a number pass to digits; and **its speaker labels are not good enough to burn a named caption** (87% overall, 72% on the guest).
  3. `scripts/transcribe_local.py` (faster-whisper, free, offline) is **last resort only**, for no network and no key. Measured on the one tape all 3 engines ran on, it found 11 of 17 confirmed proper-noun sites where Scribe found 17 and Deepgram 16, it takes no keyterms argument at all, and its word boundaries are about 3 times looser. Spot-read every proper noun before anything burns.
  All 3 cache on the FILENAME and only trust a cache their own engine wrote. A pipeline that rewrites a file under the same name must pass `--force`, or it reads back the previous run.
- `scripts/verify_speaker.py`, `scripts/check_caption_size.py` and `scripts/check_safe_area.py` are the 3 gates. They need only ffmpeg, PIL and numpy (OpenCV optional, for the face test), no key, no model call.
- For the CUT use `scripts/cutjoin.py` (NOT a generic renderer: see ERRORS).
- `hyperframes` (via `npx hyperframes render`) renders the HTML/GSAP cards. `npx` fetches it on first use; needs Node.js.
- `ffmpeg` for composition and music. **Local ffmpeg usually has no libass**, so captions are baked with PIL (`captions_brand.py`), NOT with `subtitles=`.
- **Brand:** `scripts/brand.py` reads `BRAND_ACCENT`, `BRAND_ACCENT_DARK`, `BRAND_RED`, `BRAND_NEUTRAL`, `BRAND_FONT_DISPLAY`, `BRAND_FONT_BODY` and `BRAND_CAPTION_FONT` from `content/brand.md` (then env vars, then script flags). `gen.py` and `captions_brand.py` both use it. `python3 <skill>/scripts/brand.py` prints what it resolved and from where.
- Caption font: the `.ttf` named by `BRAND_CAPTION_FONT`, else `assets/fonts/caption.ttf`, else `assets/fonts/Montserrat-VariableFont_wght.ttf` (free, SIL Open Font License). `CAPTION_FONT=/path/to/font.ttf` also works.
- Assets: see `assets/README.md`. **No fonts, logos or music ship with this skill.** Add the caption font and the whole pipeline runs; music and logos are optional.

## Machine rules (they apply here too)

A short usually renders in minutes, so these bite rarely. They cost 3 days when they did, and `compose.sh` and `gen.py` are exactly the shape that triggers them. The render docs are `content/reference/render-reliability.md`.

- **Anything over 10 minutes runs detached**: `scripts/render_detached.sh start <name> --watch <dir> -- "<command>"`, checked with `scripts/render_detached.sh status <name>`. A render started as a child of the conversation dies silently the moment the turn is interrupted or the session ends.
- **Any ffmpeg graph with a `-loop 1` still image carries an explicit output `-t`** (plus `shortest=1` on the first overlay) **and a `-framerate` that matches the video** (see ERRORS: a looped still defaults to 25 fps). A looped image never ends: ffmpeg keeps encoding duplicate frames after the video finishes, until the kernel kills it. It looks exactly like running out of memory at "98%". The tell in the log is "More than 100000 frames duplicated". `scripts/render_preflight.sh` lints every script under `.claude/skills/*/scripts/` for this.
- **While a render is running, nothing gets killed.** No Claude process, no ffmpeg, no "big one". Pause file sync and close browsers instead. Never start a second heavy job (an image generation, browser automation) against a live encode.
- **"Is it working" means the output file is growing**, never the CPU meter: `scripts/render_status.sh <dir>`.
- **A check never writes into the working directory.** The working directory is usually the project repo, and a stray PNG there gets committed. Every check here defaults to `$CLAUDE_SCRATCHPAD_DIR` or the system temp folder.
- **A long-form episode is not this skill.** That is the `long-form-editor` skill, with `content/reference/podcast-edit-runbook.md` and `content/reference/podcast-edit.md`.

## Pipeline (run in order, inside one project folder: `content/videos/<slug>/`)

0. **If you were given a reference reel (IG/TikTok link)**: study it FIRST (see "Replicate a reference reel"). Download, make a contact-sheet, sample the accent colour, then theme `gen.py` (export `BRAND_ACCENT` for this one video, or set `A` by hand).
1. **If the RAW is LANDSCAPE** (for example 4K 16:9): pre-crop to vertical 1080x1920 centered on the face, then work on `edit/raw9.mp4`:
   `ffmpeg -i <raw.MP4> -vf "crop=1215:2160:<x>:0,scale=1080:1920" -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -c:a aac edit/raw9.mp4`
   (1215 = 2160*9/16; tune `<x>` by extracting a frame and centering the face; for a 3840-wide source `x` is around 1293). Extract a frame and LOOK first: a "landscape" file can carry rotation metadata and really be vertical.
2. **Transcribe the RAW**: `python <skill>/scripts/transcribe.py <raw> --edit-dir edit --language en --keyterms-file build/keyterms.txt`
   (use `--language it` and so on for other languages. No ElevenLabs key or no credits: `scripts/transcribe_deepgram.py`, same args, same keyterms file. Neither key and no network: `scripts/transcribe_local.py`, which takes no keyterms and is last resort.)
3. **Read the transcript word-by-word** (`edit/transcripts/*.json`, print indices and times). Raw clips are often **multi-take with spoken director's notes** ("put a clip of...", "ok so...", "show the bit where..."). Those must NOT end up in the voiceover. **Hand-pick the CLEAN take of every sentence** and build an EDL of KEEP-ranges (the final narration), discarding repetitions, false starts and director's notes.
3.5. **VERIFY THE SPEAKER (gate, before the EDL exists).** Runs on **any clip line that is a self-description, a credential, or a claim**, anything the short will put in the speaker's mouth as a statement about them or as a fact they are asserting. Not needed for ordinary narration that describes no one. A transcript line proves nothing about who spoke.
   `python <skill>/scripts/verify_speaker.py <raw> --range <start>-<end> --calibration <a>-<b> [--face X,Y,W,H]`
   The calibration range is one you have already eyeballed as the target speaker clearly talking. The script crops the mouth (the lower-center third of `--face`, or a fixed lower-center crop without it), measures mean frame-to-frame pixel delta in both ranges, and reports the ratio against a threshold of **half the calibration**. Real speech scored **12-15** on the tapes it was built on, matching the incident's 13-22 band; the misattributed line scored **2.1**. Exit 1 = SUSPECT: do not write that range into the EDL until you have extracted frames and looked. `--keep DIR` keeps the crops for exactly that look.
4. **Write the EDL** by hand (a list of `ranges` with start/end of the good takes). See the format in `make_edl.py`. `make_edl.py` is fine ONLY for single-take raws; for multi-take, build the keep-list manually. **EXTEND each `end` by +0.2/0.4s** to include the word release plus a breath, otherwise you clip it (on a Deepgram transcript, take the out-point from the next word's start, not this word's end). If the transcriber collapses a multi-take zone into ONE very long "word" (a 4s blob, say), that is a red flag: inspect that window with `silencedetect`, SPLIT keeping the clean take and dropping the blob in the middle.
5. **Cut**: `python <skill>/scripts/cutjoin.py edit/edl.json edit/cut.mp4` (extract and concatenate, native resolution, no OOM).
6. **Trim residual silences** (CRITICAL): `python <skill>/scripts/silence_keep.py edit/cut.mp4 edit/edl_sil.json` (defaults: -40dB / MIN 0.35 / PAD 0.12, protects the last word; for a very punchy short add `... -40 0.30`), then `python <skill>/scripts/cutjoin.py edit/edl_sil.json edit/cutF.mp4`. **NEVER -30/-32dB**: it eats the soft word releases. Afterwards, CHECK the gaps in the re-transcribed cut: a `gap = start - prev_end` over 0.30s between 2 sentences is a pause to tighten.
7. **RE-TRANSCRIBE the final cut** (exact sync): `python <skill>/scripts/transcribe.py edit/cutF.mp4 --edit-dir edit/tF --language en --keyterms-file build/keyterms.txt`. (Fall back to `transcribe_deepgram.py`, then `transcribe_local.py`, same as step 2. Never drop to the local rung here by habit: its 0.5s boundary drift is the one recorded above, and steps 8, 10 and 11 read this file for every card trigger and caption.) Then **VERIFY no word is clipped**: the transcriber AUTO-COMPLETES cut words (it prints "output" even if the file only contains "out"). Signs of a clip: a word whose `end` exceeds the file duration, sentences that "jump", a vanishing last word. If you find one, extend that range's `end` in the EDL and re-cut. **Do NOT trust the transcript text alone.**
8. **Author the BEATS** in `cards/gen.py` (copy from `<skill>/scripts/gen.py`, or from one of the example generators in `references/examples/`) by reading `edit/tF/transcripts/cutF.json`. Each card has a `trigger` = the word or phrase it MUST appear on. Cards are **contiguous**. Timing rule: a beat lasts as long as its sentence; if you need a multi-stage sequence (logo, then price, then strike) anchor the stages to DIFFERENT words and finish BEFORE the beat's last word, otherwise the exit clips it. A copied `gen.py` still finds `brand.py` by walking up to `.claude/skills/shorts-editor/` (or set `SHORTS_EDITOR_DIR`).
9. **Project assets**: `mkdir -p cards/assets/logos && cp <skill>/assets/logos/* cards/assets/logos/` (only if you put logos there). Missing logos: find them on the brand's own press page, and read `assets/README.md` first on which marks you may actually ship. For real screenshots (an A/B comparison of 2 pages, say) resize them into `cards/assets/` and show them inside a browser-frame.
10. **Generate and render the cards**: `cd cards && python gen.py ../edit/tF/transcripts/cutF.json && npx --yes hyperframes render . -o cards_all.mp4`
11. **Patch transcriber errors, then captions**: the transcriber mangles names and terms ("Claude" becomes "Cloud", and so on; on a Deepgram transcript also turn spelled-out numbers into digits). Patch the JSON in `cutF_cap.json` (substitute on the "text" field) and generate with the brand karaoke renderer: `python <skill>/scripts/captions_brand.py edit/tF/transcripts/cutF_cap.json edit/capt 12 78` (last arg = font size; 78 is the floor and the default). It is the clip-bar style: phrase-level, sentence case, words build in as spoken, current word in `BRAND_ACCENT`. It warns when a phrase only fits under the floor: split or shorten that phrase rather than ship small type. The legacy one-word renderer (`captions.py`) stays only for A/B checks; 1 or 2 word UPPERCASE captions are out for talking lines. Both have the **soft shadow** (readable over bright B-roll, no hard stroke). The card triggers stay on the ORIGINAL transcript.
12. **Compose and music**: `bash <skill>/scripts/compose.sh edit/cutF.mp4 cards/cards_all.mp4 edit/capt renders/<name>-FINAL.mp4 <crop_y>`. For a head-and-shoulders crop (already-vertical source) crop_y varies per clip (roughly 120-300, and one clip needed 300). ALWAYS tune it on test frames (`scale=1080:-2,crop=1080:1056:0:$cy` at 3 or 4 values, contact-sheet them) so the face sits in the UPPER part of the lower band and the mouth and chin clear y=1500 in every frame, lean-ins included. "Too far down" is the most common piece of real feedback. compose puts the voice at dialog level (loudnorm) and **music at 2%** (`MUSIC_VOL=...` to change it, `MUSIC=...` to point at your track) and adds `+faststart`. **2% is the default and the shipping level.** 6% is the **retired legacy default**, named here only so nobody reinstates it thinking it is the house setting; it was called way too loud on a real review.
13. **Self-evaluate**: extract a frame (`ffmpeg -ss T -i ...`) of each schema, build a contact-sheet (PIL or `tile`) and COMPARE against the reference reels BEFORE showing it. Show only if it is decent. **Always also check**: frame 0 (the title card, not an empty band), first word (not clipped), last word plus caption (not clipped), captions readable over every B-roll, beat-to-beat transitions with no flash, **A/V sync** (`ffprobe` video stream duration roughly equal to audio, drift under 1 frame) and **caption == spoken word** (sample 3 or 4 points at mid and end: the caption must match the transcript, see the desync error).
    **Caption size is a gate before the render:** `python <skill>/scripts/check_caption_size.py --style "<the ASS Style line>" --fontsdir <fonts>`; exit 1 means raise the size. For the 375px phone read of the finished render, run the same script against the mp4 and look at the PNG it writes.
    **Safe area is a hard gate after it:** `python <skill>/scripts/check_safe_area.py renders/<name>-FINAL.mp4`. It samples the render at 1fps and fails on caption or card ink above y=200, below y=1500, or right of x=940 from y=960 down (the action rail; a card above y=960 may use the full width, `--right-from` moves it), **and on a face whose lower third (mouth, chin) sits below y=1500** (OpenCV face detection, a skin-tone fallback without it; added after a short passed with the speaker's mouth covered by the platform overlay at about 24s). It lists the timestamps and edges, and reports frames where no face was found rather than passing them silently. Exit 1 = fix the layout and re-render. **YouTube cannot swap the file on an existing video**, so a breach that ships costs a full re-upload: that is what 5 re-uploads in one day once were. The ink test is deliberately approximate and bright-ink only (default `--bright 245`, calibrated so a lit wall or a white shirt in the footage band is not flagged); drop it to around 200 to sweep harder and eyeball the hits. `--keep DIR` keeps the frames, with the face box and the bottom line drawn on every flagged one.
    **Then hand off to the `cut-review` skill.** Self-evaluate clears this skill's own mechanics; `cut-review` is the review gate and runs on the finished short **before anyone watches it**, whatever question is still open about it (an open question rides with the review, it never replaces it). A short that has passed self-evaluate has not been reviewed.
14. **Retro and self-learning** (MANDATORY, see the section below): when the reel is done, say what went well and what went badly, and update THIS skill with the lessons.

## Style (the brand layer: `content/brand.md` is the truth)

Everything visual is tokens. `scripts/brand.py` reads them from `content/brand.md`; swap the accent there and every short re-themes. The structure below is the proven default, not a law, and the person whose taste it is may reject it.

- 9:16, talking-head in the bottom 55% or so (overlay y=864), top band 0-864 black for the schemas.
- **ONE accent colour per video** on black: `{BRAND_ACCENT}` (tokens `A`/`AD`/`GL` in `gen.py` derive from it), `{BRAND_ACCENT_DARK}` for fills and secondary elements. Keep `{BRAND_RED}` (default `#ff5470`) for "no / lost / expensive" and `{BRAND_NEUTRAL}` (default steel `#7f93ad`) for a neutral competitor. A sponsored video may theme the accent to the sponsor's colour via the reference-reel flow.
- Card type: `{BRAND_FONT_DISPLAY}` with `{BRAND_FONT_BODY}` as the fallback, as CSS font families in the card HTML. Make sure the font is installed or loaded in the card page, or the render falls back to a system face.
- Captions per the clip bar (`content/taste/clip-bar.md`): **phrase-level, sentence case, slow-build word-by-word in sync with speech, current word highlighted in the accent**. Rendered by `captions_brand.py` (the caption font at weight 700, white, soft shadow, no stroke, lower third at the band edge). The 1 or 2 word UPPERCASE style is out for talking lines; a big title-card flourish is allowed only on a cold-open stat.
- **Caption floor: Montserrat 78px at 1080 wide (a capital H 35px tall).** 52px was far too small in the hand. The floor once lived in a single episode's build script instead of here, and 12 of 15 renders repeated the miss 3 days later. Gate every caption style with `check_caption_size.py` before a render. A review or a research finding that wants captions smaller or bigger is reported as a conflict for a person to settle; it never moves the number on its own. With a different caption font, keep the capital-H height at 35px or more, not the point size.
- Calibrate motion to the audience. For a serious or professional audience: **no bounces, spins or glows on content elements**, and the decorative layer stays subtle. For a consumer audience you can push it. Your `content/taste/editing-rules.md` says which.
- Decorative layer ALWAYS on: rising particles, streaks, a breathing glow, a scrolling grid. The top band is never empty or static.
- Big schemas that FILL the band (keyword around 112px, numbers around 200px, tall graphs). **Nothing under 44px ever ships**, see the ERRORS row on phone-sized type. The band is read at 375px wide.
- **Music bed at 2%**, which is `compose.sh`'s own default (`MUSIC_VOL=0.02`). 6% is the retired legacy default and is named only so it is not reinstated.
- **Face-forward with generated cards is the format.** A deck-over-voice variant built from a real presentation has been tried and loses. Do not rebuild it.
- First frame = the packaged promise (clip-bar): a designed title card in your brand type and colours, on its own high track, holding around 0.95s before it fades to reveal beat 0. Direction per `content/taste/thumbnails.md`.

## Replicate a reference reel (when you are given a link)

1. **Download**: `yt-dlp -o ref.%(ext)s "<reel url>"` (IG and TikTok both work).
2. **Study it closely**: contact-sheet at 2fps with ffmpeg `tile` (`ffmpeg -ss A -t 15 -i ref.mp4 -vf "fps=2,scale=216:384,tile=5x6:padding=4:color=black" sheetA.jpg`) and READ it. Extract: layout (where the talking-head sits, where the schemas go), the VOCABULARY of schemas used (terminal? grid? versus? count-up? command-bar?), the rhythm (how often the graphic changes), the caption style.
3. **Sample the accent**: extract a frame with a vivid colour and read the bright pixels (PIL), then theme this video with `BRAND_ACCENT=#RRGGBB` (or set `A` in `gen.py`).
4. **Map** your beats onto the schemas observed in the reference. Do NOT copy the reference's text: use YOUR content.

## User B-roll in the top band (over the face)

Sometimes you will get clips (a screen-recording of a site, a report, a list) to show "over the face" while talking about them. They go in the top band (0-864), in their time window, ON TOP of the cards.

- **Map the windows** to the words (report on "report", site on "website", list on "skills"). Each window = `enable='between(t,A,B)'`.
- **Cover the card** underneath with a black band for the whole B-roll window (`color=black:s=1080x864` overlay with `enable`), extended to the START of the next beat so the card does not reappear at the edges.
- **Framing**: a landscape or screen clip goes full-bleed `scale=-2:864,crop=1080:864` (fills the band, no letterbox); a portrait clip (a document) goes `scale=-2:824` centered. Dark B-roll blends into the black; for bright ones rely on the subtitle shadow.
- **Clip timing**: `[N:v]trim=0:DUR,setpts=PTS-STARTPTS,fps=25,scale=...,setpts=PTS+A/TB[bv]` then `overlay` with enable in the window. Full example: `references/examples/compose-broll.sh`.

## ERRORS NOT TO REPEAT (learned the hard way)
| Error | Rule |
|--------|--------|
| Card = text inside a box | NO. Use **schemas/graphs/summary phrases**. The WORDS are carried by the subtitles; the cards carry the VISUAL. |
| Boxes that pulse on every word | NEVER per-word pop-scale on the cards. Movement = snappy entrances + continuous decorative layer + self-drawing graphs. |
| Sync computed on paper | It accumulates drift. **ALWAYS re-transcribe the final cut** and anchor cards+subtitles to the real word times. |
| Trimming only word-gaps | Leaves 0.6-1.1s silences. ALWAYS run `silence_keep.py` (silencedetect on the audio). |
| Graphics only at the top | Fill the whole top band + animated decorative layer. |
| Black outline on subtitles | Clean white only, no stroke. |
| Sparse cards (1 every 5s) | Cards **contiguous**, one always present, each on the word that triggers it. |

| "you-plural / everyone" subtitles | Use singular second person consistently (the speaker's voice). |
| Showing half-baked versions | Self-evaluate on frames vs reference; come back only if it's decent. |
| **Keeping director's notes in the voiceover** | Raws are multi-take with spoken notes ("put a clip", "ok so"). Hand-pick the clean take of each sentence (keep-EDL), drop the rest. |
| **A generic renderer for the cut** | A renderer that resolves the source path relative to the EDL folder (giving `edit/edit/...`) and forces `scale=1920` will OOM on many segments. Use `cutjoin.py`. |
| **Music too loud** | Old default 0.15 covered the voice; 0.06 replaced it and was still called way too loud on review. compose now: voice loudnorm -16, **music 0.02**. Both 0.15 and 0.06 are retired defaults. Measure levels (`volumedetect`) if the voice was recorded low. |
| **File won't open (QuickTime/IG)** | Needs `+faststart` (moov atom up front). Already in compose.sh; if you remux by hand: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -c copy -movflags +faststart out.mp4`. |
| **Subtitles with transcriber typos** | Patch "Cloud" to "Claude" and the like in the CAPTION JSON before generating (card triggers stay on the original transcript). Better still, put the names in `build/keyterms.txt` so the transcriber hears them right in the first place. |
| **Multi-stage sequence in a short beat** | Logo, then price, then strike won't fit if the trigger is the last word. Anchor stages to different words and close before the beat exit. |
| **Clipping words (THE MOST EXPENSIVE)** | At -30dB the silence-trimmer eats soft tails, turning "output" into "out" and costing 3 rounds of fixes. Rule: silence_keep defaults (-40dB, last word protected) + extended EDL `end`s + **VERIFY the cut's AUDIO**, NOT the transcript alone (it auto-completes cut words). |
| **find() grabs the FIRST occurrence** | A word that occurs earlier (in the hook AND the next sentence, say) starts the beat on the wrong occurrence (animation too early). Use `find_after(trig, after)` to anchor to the right one. |
| **White subtitle on white B-roll** | A bright screen-recording (a site) plus a white subtitle is illegible. Both caption renderers now have the soft shadow; also send bright B-roll full-bleed so the subtitle sits at the dark bottom edge. |
| **TextPlugin for the typewriter** | TextPlugin writes to innerHTML, so it eats tags like `<role>` (it parses them as HTML). To type text with `<` and `>` use a tween on a counter + `textContent=val.slice(0,n)`. |
| **Count-up in a tiny beat** | A count-up from 0 to 91,000 in 0.9s is unreadable: if the beat lasts under 1.2s, POP the final number (scale-in), no counting. |
| **Card under the B-roll reappearing at the edges** | When a B-roll covers a card in its window, the faded card can flash 1-2 frames when the B-roll detaches. Cover with a black band (`enable=between`) up to the start of the next beat, not just to the end of the B-roll. |
| **Dupes/false-starts hidden in one long word** | The RAW transcriber collapses repeated takes into ONE long word (a 2s "save" hiding a repeated phrase; an "I'll ma--" before "I'll make"). They only show up by re-transcribing the cut: after cutF, SCAN the transcript for consecutive duplicate phrases and micro-truncations (`ma--`, `us--`), then `silencedetect` the original window to find the split, divide the range dropping the dupe, re-cut. Dropping director's notes from the RAW isn't enough. |
| **A/V desync that grows mid/late clip** | Concat-COPY of AAC segments (old `cutjoin.py`: `concat -c copy`) accumulates encoder priming per segment, so audio drifts from video, visible after about 40s (mouth not aligned, so subtitles aren't either). FIX: `cutjoin.py` now JOINS segments with the **concat FILTER** (`[0:v][0:a]...concat=n=N:v=1:a=1`), which re-decodes and concatenates decoded streams for sample-accurate sync. Runs on short segments, no OOM. Verify: `ffprobe` video stream duration roughly equal to audio (drift under 1 frame). |
| **A logo SVG that was broken** | github.svg once had `viewBox 0 0 1024 1024` but path coords 0-16, so it rendered invisible. Verify ALL logos on a black background before rendering: no fill = black-on-black. A `currentColor` logo (openai.svg, for one) loaded via `<img>` is BLACK: bake `fill="#fff"`. |
| **Inter-sentence pauses too long (short not punchy)** | Old `silence_keep.py` default (MIN 0.5, PAD 0.16) left 0.3-0.5s pauses, which drew "too much pause" feedback. Default now MIN **0.35**, PAD_AFTER **0.12** (word tails still protected by -40dB + PAD_LAST 0.45). For a very punchy short pass `MIN 0.30`. After the cut CHECK the gaps in the transcript (`gap = start - prev_end`): if over 0.30s between 2 sentences, tighten. |
| **Key visual entering on the LAST word** | The main element of a card anchored to the beat's last word leaves the card half-empty for 2-3s and "appears late". Anchor the key visual EARLY in the beat (one of the first words or `s+0.2`), not the last; keep it present for the whole beat. |
| **User image with window chrome "baked" in** | Screenshots of windows have the light window border INSIDE the PNG: on a black band it makes an ugly edge. Detect the frame via PIL (bbox of non-cream pixels) and CROP the inside; then on black add an **accent border** via CSS (`border:4px solid {A}`) to separate the dark logo from the black. |
| **Spoken triplets repeated identically** | If the RAW repeats the SAME sentence 3 times ("use hooks, use hooks, use hooks") you usually want to keep ONE in the cut (the cleanest or most emphatic take), not all 3. Different from content rule-of-three (lists/numbers): keep those. When in doubt, one. |
| **A bare number stat = sterile** | A big static number ("1000+ HOURS") is flat. Make it a VISUAL: a curve or area that draws itself (svg path + gradient + glow) with the number popping at the peak. Applies to hours, percentages and counts. |
| **"Landscape" source that's actually vertical** | ffprobe can report 1920x1080 while a rotation side_data makes the clip VERTICAL (phone/camera metadata; ffmpeg auto-rotates on decode). Extract a frame and LOOK at it before pre-cropping: a needless crop=1215:2160 here would have failed or mangled it. If the frame is portrait, skip step 1 entirely. |
| **silence_keep finds 0 silences (noisy room)** | With a noise floor above -40dB, silence_keep's default threshold detects nothing and long emphasis pauses (0.5-1.1s) survive. Diagnose with `silencedetect=n=-35dB:d=0.35`, then hand-write a tight keep-EDL on the cut (pad around 0.15s around each detected window, protect word tails) and re-cut plus re-transcribe. Don't blindly lower silence_keep to -30/-32 (it eats releases). |
| **Silent lead at the HEAD = stray video, not just dead air** | The transcriber routinely gives the first word `start: 0.00` when the voice actually begins several hundred ms later, so the head looks tight in the transcript and the clip opens on whatever the speaker was doing before they spoke. A 0.75s lead once shipped a hairflip at the head of a short; a person caught it, the transcript never could. Always `silencedetect` the finished cut and read the FIRST `silence_end` as the true voice onset, then extract frames at 0.0/0.3/0.6 and LOOK. Same check on the tail. |
| **Word-index anchors without a guard** | Anchoring cards to word INDICES is precise but one miscounted index desyncs a beat silently. Put an `EXPECT={idx:"word"}` assert block at the top of gen.py that verifies every anchor word before building the timeline. It catches off-by-ones at generation time instead of in the rendered video. |
| **A face crop set once for a whole tape** | The speaker MOVES. One global crop set for a whole tape came out 50-100px off per clip, and in a 2.93x upscale that is a face jammed against the frame edge. Measure the face centre PER CLIP. 2 automatic detectors (mic-darkness centroid, skin-tone run) both lied confidently: the mic is a fixed object the speaker moves away from, and the skin rule locks onto hands and warm walls. The only reliable read was a **ruled contact sheet** (gridlines every 32px, labelled) that a human eye reads off. Do that, it takes one round trip. |
| **Frame 0 is an empty band + one caption word** | Beats fade in over about 0.2s and captions build word by word, so frame 0 of a freshly composed short is black with a single word on it, a straight break of the first-frame rule (the first frame is the packaged promise). Ship a **title card on its own high track**, `tl.set(opacity:1)` at 0, holding around 0.95s, then fading to reveal beat 0 underneath. Always extract frame 0 and LOOK before calling a short done. |
| **The card-type class also styles the wrapper** | `<div class="inner {t}">` means a rule like `.stamp{border:...}` or `.weekcal{display:grid}` hits the wrapper as well as the card, drawing a phantom border round the whole band or breaking the column layout. Namespace it: `class="inner c-{t}"`. |
| **`\n` in card copy renders as a space** | HTML collapses newlines, so a deliberately stacked keyword comes out as one over-wide line. Put `white-space:pre-line` on every display-type class (`.kw`, `.unit`, `.strk`, `.strkin`, `.tline`). |
| **A generic second-pass renderer doubles the path** | `silence_keep.py` writes the source as the path it was handed, so a resolver that joins that whole relative path to the EDL folder produces `edit/<project>/.../cut.mp4`. Join the **basename** only, the way `cutjoin.py` does. |
| **A row list that overflows into the caption zone** | 7 invoice rows at default padding ran to y=724; captions start at 718, so the last row sat behind the text. Any list over about 5 rows needs a compact variant (smaller padding, font, gap), or check the band height before authoring it. |
| **Card type sized for a laptop, not a phone** | The band is 1080x864 inside a 1080x1920 frame and it is read at phone size: a 25px label is 9px on a 375px-wide screen. This was caught on a 7-row list that looked tidy in review and was unreadable in the hand. **Minimums: headline 96px, number 180px, supporting label 44px.** 6 words max per card, no per-row label+chip lists. **New gate:** render one frame per card cropped to the band and scaled to 375px wide, then read it. If you cannot, the card is wrong. |
| **A count-up that finishes under the title card** | The title card holds around 0.95s and fades over 0.32s, so a beat-0 count-up runs entirely hidden and the number is mid-flight the instant it is revealed ("28" instead of "30"). Same for any beat under about 2.2s. Force the pop treatment on beat 0 and on short beats: a `pop` flag beats guessing at durations. |
| **A quiet bed sounds absent in a noisy room, but isn't** | Measuring music level in a speech gap is confounded by room tone: at 2% the gap read identically to no-music, which looks like the bed vanished. It had not. **Isolate the bed** by mixing the render against a no-music render with one inverted (`volume=-1` + `amix`): the voice cancels and what remains is the bed alone. That showed -32.1 dB at 6% against -41.1 dB at 2%, exactly the 9 dB the change predicts. |
| **npx hyperframes ETARGET flake** | The npm registry can transiently 404 the latest hyperframes version ("No matching version found"). The package is already in the npx cache: run it directly via `find ~/.npm/_npx -name hyperframes -type d`, then `<that>/node_modules/.bin/hyperframes render ...`. |
| **Ink outside YouTube's safe area** | Card ink at y=96, captions ending at y=1680 and text reaching x=1014 under the action rail, all at once. 5 videos had to be re-uploaded, because YouTube cannot swap the file on an existing video. Run `check_safe_area.py` on every render before it goes anywhere (its bottom line is now y=1500, see the face row below). |
| **A caption size that lives in one episode's script** | The 78px caption floor was set once, written into a single build script, and 12 of 15 renders repeated the miss 3 days later. A number a person corrected belongs in the skill, gated by a script (`check_caption_size.py`), not in the file that happened to be open. |
| **Attributing a line from the transcript alone** | A line another person said on the tape reads in whisper exactly like the on-camera speaker saying it. Run `verify_speaker.py` on any self-description, credential or claim before it reaches the EDL: mouth motion scored 2.1 on the misattributed line against 13-22 on real speech. |
| **The safe-area gate passed a covered mouth** | The ink test only sees bright graphic pixels, and skin is mid-tone, so a short whose speaker sat too low passed with the mouth under the platform's bottom overlay at about 24s. `check_safe_area.py` now draws the bottom line at y=1500 (the bottom 22% is covered on every platform) and runs a face-in-band test: the largest face per frame, failing when its lower third (mouth, chin) is below the line. Lift the face, less headroom on top, and fill the bottom with a faded extension. |
| **The right-rail test ran at every height** | The action rail is dead only from 50% of the height down. Testing the right 16% at every height forced an 800px-wide board panel that was unreadable on a phone. The gate's right test now starts at y=960 (`--right-from`), so a card above the rail may use the full width. Captions still end at 1500 and the top 12% stays clear. |
| **A looped still input runs at 25 fps** | `-loop 1 -i mask.png` defaults to 25 fps, so an `alphamerge` against 30 fps video stretched every masked window by 20% and a later window landed 5.9s late. Give every looped still `-framerate 30` (or whatever the video runs at), and pad a tail with `tpad=stop=<frames>` after an `fps=30`, because `stop_duration` added nothing after a `concat`. |
| **A builder reused the old cut because the segments matched** | A builder that caches `clip.mp4` whenever the segment list matches will happily caption and ship the OLD picture after you re-lay the picture on the same timeline, with no warning. Any pre-composer that rewrites the source sets the old cached cut aside (rename it, for example `clip-stale.mp4`) before the builder runs. |
| **A check wrote its PNGs into the repo** | `check_caption_size.py <render>` once fell back to `.` when no scratch folder was set and dropped 2 PNGs in the project root, minutes from a commit. It now falls back to the system temp folder, and any new check does the same. |
| **A transcript cache that is another engine's words** | All 3 transcribers write `<stem>.json` to the same folder. A cache check that only asks "does the file exist" hands back the fallback's words under the route's name. Each script now trusts a cache only when its own engine's name is in the `_engine` header, and re-transcribes otherwise. Pass `--force` after rewriting a file under the same name. |
| **Cutting on Deepgram word ends** | Deepgram's raw word end runs into the next word's start (90% of its inter-word gaps are exactly 0), so a cut taken from it lands up to 160ms late. `transcribe_deepgram.py` snaps ends to measured energy by default, which removes the systematic late bias; still prefer starts for out-points. And patch its spelled-out numbers to digits before captions. |

## Self-learning (MANDATORY at the end of a reel)

The goal is to **one-shot** the reel. Every edit must make the skill better. When the reel is delivered and approved (or after the last feedback round):

1. **Honest retro in chat**: briefly, say **what went well** and **what went badly** in THIS reel (where you cut it, which bugs, how many rounds, what you should have anticipated). No self-praise: the useful part is the error.
2. **Distill the lesson into a rule**: if the error is repeatable, update this skill WITHOUT asking.
   - A recurring technical error becomes a row in the **ERRORS NOT TO REPEAT** table plus a fix in the right script (`silence_keep.py`, `captions_brand.py`, `gen.py`, `check_safe_area.py` and so on).
   - A new technique or animation requested becomes an entry in `references/animation-library.md` (with the snippet) and in the **Schema vocabulary**.
   - A style or content preference goes in `content/taste/shorts-formats.md` or `content/taste/editing-rules.md`, never only in one video's build script. A number a person corrected belongs where the next short will read it.
3. **Update the examples**: add the new project's generator to `references/examples/` so the next reel starts from a better reference.

Over time the errors table and the animation-library get rich enough to do it all on the first try. Every piece of feedback not repeated twice is a skill converging to one-shot.

## Notes

- `hyperframes render` outputs `yuv420p` (no alpha), which is why cards run on a black band and get cropped (0-864). Fine, because the top band is already black.
- yt-dlp for downloading YouTube audio or music needs **deno** plus an up-to-date yt-dlp, otherwise you get a 403. For reference IG reels yt-dlp works directly.
- Reference reels: keep your own 3 to 5 in `content/taste/shorts-formats.md`, each with one line on what to take from it. `references/style.md` says how to use them.
- Keyterms: `build/keyterms.txt` in the video folder, one name per line (people, products, places on the tape). Names go into the transcriber, never into a find-and-replace afterwards.
- Working example generators are in `references/examples/` (their on-screen copy is invented English sample text, kept for the card code; their colours are hard-coded per example on purpose):
  - `gen-reel1-skills.py` (**green, 4 tools**: staged hook LOGO then stamp, count-up plus 4 numbered blocks anchored with `find_after`, skilltitle 01-04, per-word pills with emoji plus result, **TYPEWRITER terminal** messy to clean, bigstat pop "91,000+", coloured-word CTA. **3 user B-roll clips in the top band**, see `compose-broll.sh`. An example of the anti-clipping pipeline.)
  - `gen-reel2-claude.py` (**orange, "coding tool tricks"**: 14 beats, landscape 4K source to raw9. New cards: claudehero, asciiwin, staged terminal plus questions, staged terminal plus vulnerability, mdfile, githubcard, risinggraph, triple-pulse hooks, twopill.
