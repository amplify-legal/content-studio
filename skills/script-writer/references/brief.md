# The brief a script serves

A brief comes in 2 parts, and they do different jobs.

- **Part 1, the research brief.** Written before the interview. It is the reaction surface: the
  creator reads it, argues with it out loud, and what they say gets written down verbatim. The
  fuller version of this template is `content/frameworks/research-brief.md`.
- **Part 2, the interview record.** Written during and after the interview, at
  `content/briefs/<date>-<slug>.md`. This is what the script serves. It is the record of a
  conversation with the creator, not a document written for them.

The script is built from what they said, not from what the research brief guessed.

---

# Part 1. The research brief

Copy this into the video's own folder and fill it in. 6 sections plus the goal.

---

## 00. Goal

Pick one. It decides the mid-roll and the end call to action, and nothing else in the brief
makes sense until it is picked.

- **Conversions.** The video exists to move people into a paid thing or a community.
- **Awareness.** Reach. Broaden the top of the funnel.
- **Authority.** Technical credibility with people who already know the space.
- **Broadening.** Pull one audience they have into a second thing they do.

Then 1 or 2 sentences on why this goal for this video.

---

## 01. The concept

**Shape of this video, in one sentence.** For example: a dense numbered list of 5 moves with
before-and-after cuts, or a walkthrough built on one mental model, or a decision tree with one
rule per section, or the story of what N hours of doing something taught them.

That sentence is the constraint. The vision and the structure both flow from it, so write it
before anything else.

**Working title.** Concrete, in their words. No hype adjectives.

**What it covers.** The simplest end to end walkthrough of the topic: the core pieces, the
simplest way to assemble them, the build step by step, the one mistake beginners make, and what
to do once it works.

**Who it is for.** Specific. "Anybody who has watched 5 tutorials on this, opened 10 tabs, and
still cannot picture how the pieces connect" beats "beginners".

**Reference video.** Title, channel, view count, link, and 1 or 2 sentences on why that format
carries this topic.

**What you are taking from it.** Structure, never topic. 3 to 5 elements, and match the count to
what you actually take rather than padding to 5. Things worth borrowing: a hook scaffold, the
pacing of one cause per section, a title number that works as a contract with the viewer,
building live on screen with the simplest possible stack, paying the build off with a real run
so the viewer feels the moment it works.

---

## 02. The vision

One paragraph on the differentiated angle, plus the feel.

Most content in any space is either shallow or unreadable. Name what this one is instead, in a
sentence that could only describe this video. Then the feel in a few words (calm and precise,
warm and fast, no hype), what is on screen when they talk versus when they build, and what the
viewer should be thinking as they leave.

---

## 03. The hook, first draft

Mirror the reference video's hook structure. This is a draft they react to and edit live, never
a finished hook.

- The opening line: what the video promises, in one sentence.
- The pain line: why most attempts at this topic fail. Same register as the audience.
- The promise line: a concrete deliverable, and the reassurance that a beginner can follow it.
- An optional proof line, only if the reference has one. Soft, single sentence.
- The transition into the body.

Then list what they have to confirm or kill: the proof line, any word too complex for this
audience, every number.

---

## 04. Mid-roll

Strategy: spoken call to action, natural placement, or skip.

**Skip is a real choice**, and it is the default for authority videos and anything where the
teaching is the value. A forced mid-roll breaks the trust the rest of the video built.

For natural placement: the trigger moment (where the thing appears as the answer to a problem
the viewer just felt), what is on screen for 15 to 30 seconds, and how the teaching resumes.

For a spoken one: the pain that primes it, the destination, and 1 sentence in their voice. No
"click below", no urgency they do not mean.

---

## 05. End call to action, first draft

The end points at another video on their channel, not at the product. Draft 2 options and let
them pick one in the interview.

Each option: the leading sentence, the destination video, and the link. If the destination does
not exist yet, mark it "to make next" and lock the slot anyway.

---

## 06. What the interview has to come back with

This is the part that decides whether the script is theirs or yours.

- **The pieces in their words**, with the analogy they reach for under pressure for each one.
  Ask it straight: "what's the analogy that makes this click?"
- **The build sequence** on screen, in the order a beginner should follow it.
- **1 or 2 real mistake stories**, from their actual work, with what happened.
- **What to do once it works**, in their framing.
- **Their reaction to the hook.** Confirm, edit, or kill.
- **Mid-roll specifics.** What is on screen.
- **The end call to action pick.** Option A or B, and the destination.
- **The transitions.** For each joint: "what makes someone need piece 2 after piece 1?"

Capture verbatim. Filler and false starts included. Tidying their sentences is how a script
stops sounding like them.

**One setup note worth passing on:** dictate the interview instead of typing it. Speaking
preserves the voice that the whole thing depends on, and it takes a third of the time.

---

## Appendix. Known shapes

Cross-check the sentence from section 01 against these. If it matches one, that pattern tells
you the pacing. If it matches none, write the shape in your own words and let the brief adapt.

| Shape | What it is | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Metaphor walkthrough** | One sustained mental model carries the whole teach, with a matching illustration set | The topic has clean structural pieces and the audience is new to it |
| **Numbered list** | The title declares a number, the body delivers exactly that count, 45 to 90 seconds each, one cause and effect per item | The topic is a set of discrete tactical moves. The number is the contract |
| **Time deal** | "Give me X minutes and I will..." on top of a numbered body | The outcome is the draw rather than the count |
| **Decision tree** | "When to use X versus Y versus Z", a series of rules rather than a sequential teach | The audience already knows the options exist and is stuck choosing |
| **Memoir** | "I spent N hours doing this, here is what I learned." 5 to 8 lessons with stories, not a tutorial | The audience trusts them already, and the lessons need the story to land |

# Part 2. The interview record

Copy this to `content/briefs/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md`. The promise, the spine, what to leave out
and the packaging are the creator's. The retrieval around them (timestamps, sources, the number
check) is the machine's. Derivatives are planned before recording, so clipping later is
retrieval rather than a scavenge.

**A brief with `OPEN:` lines in it is fine, and often honest. A brief that filled the creator's
sections with good guesses is not.** Anything they did not settle reads `OPEN:` followed by the
question, and stays that way until they answer.

```
# Brief: <working title> (<slug>)

Interview: full (date) / skipped, "just draft it" / re-run of (date)
Shaped by: <who answered>
Record date: YYYY-MM-DD · Launch slot: YYYY-MM-DD · Type: flagship / guest / workshop
```

## The promise, in their words

The sentence a viewer would use to describe this video to a friend. Not a title. Whatever they
actually said, verbatim. If they picked from framings you offered, note which one and why.

## The spine, in their order

The pieces in the order they would actually teach them. Their sequencing is the argument, so
record the order they gave and any reason they gave for it. If this is part of a series, note
what has to exist before it.

Keep 2 columns, so their wording survives beside yours. Theirs goes on camera.

## What they decided to leave out

The cut nobody asks for and everybody needs. Often the most valuable line in the brief. Ask
what they would cut **and** what they would keep that a shorter version would drop; a question
shaped as pure subtraction gets a worse answer.

## Their honest read

Anything they said about the idea that is not a decision: what they are unsure about, what they
think is weak, what they want to try. Verbatim. This is what stops a later session from
re-litigating something they already worried about.

## The gate answers

A conversation, not a form. An honest no is worth hearing early. The 3 questions live in
`content/frameworks/youtube-pillar.md`; record each answer with where it came from (a real
person who asked this, the number it moves, who you would send it to).

## Lines they want verbatim

Co-created in the interview, never planted by the machine. Each one in full, in their words,
and written into its section of the script as well. Clip candidates are still picked from the
recording afterwards; a line on this list is a hope, not a guaranteed short.

## Call to action plan (asked, never prefilled)

What is the ask, where does it sit, and is there a resource behind it? A framework's default
percentage is only what the question offers; their answer outranks it everywhere. Record their
words and the placement they chose. Unanswered means `OPEN:` with the question, never a guess.

**Guest episodes carry 3 more answers**, asked before the recording rather than after the edit:
the guest's own distribution (a list, a group, a community), your call to action into their
audience, and the call to action they want shown on screen, with the exact handle or address.

## Packaging candidates

- Titles, 3 to 5.
- The thumbnail board: rendered, never described. Each render captioned with one of the titles
  above, because the title and the thumbnail are one story. Their pick goes here.

## Vision handoff (filled right after recording)

The creator records about 10 minutes straight after the shoot: what the great version looks like,
the feeling, the cuts they already know, what matters most. The transcript goes here. Nobody
edits blind, and `cut-review` checks the cut against this section.

## Post-record notes

- Verbatim lines that landed on tape, with timestamps.
- Every promise made on camera ("link below", "a free template"), each with the URL that has to
  be live in the description before launch.
- Anything said on tape that needs a number or fact check before launch.
