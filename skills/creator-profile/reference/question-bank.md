# The question bank

> Read by `creator-profile` before each section. This is a bank, not a script. Ask the opening question, listen, and pick the next one from what they actually said. Most sections need 4 to 6 questions, not all of them. Never read a list out. Never put 2 questions in one message.

How each section is laid out:

- **Trying to learn:** what the section must leave behind. When you have it, stop.
- **Open with:** the first question.
- **Then, if it is still open:** follow-ups, in no fixed order.
- **When the answer is generic:** the one push-back. Use it once.
- **Never ask:** the questions that look useful and do damage.

---

## 1. Who they are, and how they want to come across

**Trying to learn:** who they are in their own sentence; how they want to be described; what they refuse to be associated with; what people already praise them for; the belief they hold that most of their space does not; 3 to 5 moments that made them.

**Open with:** "If a friend of yours had to explain to someone what you do, in one breath, what would you want them to say?"

**Then, if it is still open:**

- "Someone just finished one of your videos and is telling a friend about you. What do you hope they say?"
- "What would you hate to be lumped in with? A type of creator, a tone, a kind of promise." Then: "Why that one?"
- "What do people already thank you for, or tell you you're good at, without you asking?" Ask for the last time it happened.
- "What do you believe about your subject that most people in your space would argue with?"
- "What are 3 moments, good or bad, that are the reason you do this?" Take them one at a time and ask what changed after each.
- "Is there anything about you that should never come up on camera?" Write the answer as a rule in `who.md`, never as a reason.

**When the answer is generic** ("helpful", "authentic", "real", "I just want to add value"): "What is the last thing you made or said that someone would point at as proof of that?"

**Never ask:** "What are your 3 brand adjectives?" Labels are not answers, and a label written into a file reads as settled when it is not.

---

## 2. The business

Ask the privacy question before the first money question: "Prices, revenue and what a customer is worth: do those go in the profile file, or in a private file that stays out of git?" Then do what they say.

**Trying to learn:** each offer; who buys it; the price band; the real path from viewer to customer; what one customer is worth; how many new customers they can take; what content must never sell.

**Open with:** "What do you sell today, if anything? All of it, even the small things."

**Then, if it is still open:**

- "Who buys that? Describe the last person who did, by what they do, not by name."
- "Roughly what does it cost them? A band is fine: under 100, 100 to 1,000, 1,000 to 10,000, more. Or 'rather not say'." Offer the bands; never pick one for them.
- "Think of your last 3 customers. How did each one first find you, and what happened between that and paying?" Write each path as steps.
- "Of everyone who watches or reads your content, what do you hope the next step is? A follow, a newsletter, a message, a booked call, a purchase?"
- "Over the time someone stays a customer, what is one customer worth to you? If you don't know, that is a fine answer."
- "How many new customers could you take on in a month before something breaks?"
- "Is there anything your content should never sell, promise or imply?"
- "Is there an offer you're thinking about but haven't launched?" Write it as `proposed`.

**When the answer is generic** ("anyone who wants to grow", "everyone"): "Who was the last person who paid you? What were they doing the week before they found you?"

**No business yet:** "Is the plan to sell something one day, or is the content the point?" Then record what they said and move to objectives. Never suggest an offer.

**Never ask:** "What is your revenue?" They did not come here for an audit. And never do the arithmetic for them (price times customers): a number they did not say is a number you invented.

## 3. Objectives, broad and specific

**Trying to learn:** the 12-month picture; what content is for, in order; the 90-day number, where it is read, today's value, the target, the date; the one metric the system moves week to week.

**Open with:** "It's a year from now and this year of content was worth it. What happened?"

**Then, if it is still open:**

- "Is the content mainly for building an audience, for bringing in customers, for a launch, for your name, or for a community? If 2 of those pull in different directions, which wins?"
- "A video that gets a lot of views and no customers, or one that gets few views and 2 customers. Which one was the better week for you?"
- "In the next 90 days, what one number would tell you this is working?"
- "Where do you read that number today? And what is it right now, if you know?"
- "What would the number be on day 90 for you to call it a win?" A hedged target is written as `proposed`.
- "What will you stop doing to make room for this?"
- Then propose the one metric the system moves, and the reason, once: "The system can't move your revenue directly, but it can move how many pieces you ship a week and how many viewers reach your next step. I'd track [one of those]. What would you track?" Write theirs.

**When the answer is generic** ("grow", "get more followers"): "Grow to what, by when, and what changes for you when you get there?"

**Never ask:** "What is your 5-year vision?" It produces a speech, not a target. And never set the target for them.

---

## 4. Style, read off their own content

This section is mostly reading and playback. The questions are few.

**Trying to learn:** their repeated phrases, their opening and closing moves, their pacing, their energy on camera, and what their best pieces do that their worst does not, each one confirmed or corrected by them.

**Open with:** "Send me 3 to 10 of your own pieces, links or files. 2 you're proud of, 2 that are typical, and 1 you don't like. I'll read or watch them and tell you what I hear."

**While you read:** tell them how long it will take in 1 line, and that they can keep talking about anything else meanwhile. Never make them wait in silence.

**The playback, one observation per message, each with its receipt:**

- "In 4 of the 6, you open by asking the viewer something (0:02 in the first, 0:00 in the third). Is that on purpose?"
- "You say 'here's the thing' in 3 pieces, always right before your main point. Keep it as yours, or is it a tic you'd like gone?"
- "Your proud pieces run at about 170 words a minute. The one you don't like runs at about 125, with long pauses. I counted words in the transcript over minutes of speech. Does slower feel wrong to you, or was something else off that day?"
- "In the frames you're looking at the lens almost the whole time in the 2 you're proud of, and down at notes in the one you don't like. Does that match how they felt to record?"

**After each answer:** write it. Confirmed goes to `voice.md` (marked confirmed), corrected is written as they said it, "not sure" stays in `style.md` as heard, not confirmed.

**Never ask:** "How would you describe your style?" Most people cannot, and the answer is how they wish they sounded. Read first, then ask about what you read.

---

## 5. Inspirations

**Trying to learn:** 3 to 10 creators or pieces they love and why, 3 they dislike and why; for each, the move to steal and the move to avoid, in their words.

**Open with:** "Who are 3 creators, or 3 specific pieces, that you wish you'd made? Links if you have them."

**Then, for each one:**

- "What is it about this one, specifically? Not the topic, the thing they do."
- After your teardown: "Here's what I'd steal: [the move, with its timestamp]. Is that what drew you, or was it something else?"

**Then the dislikes:**

- "Now 3 that put you off, in your space or near it. What do they do that you never want to do?"
- "Is it the move itself you dislike, or how they do it?" (A move done badly can still be stolen.)

**When the answer is generic** ("their energy", "they're just good"): "Show me the exact moment. Where in the piece did you feel it?"

**Never ask:** "Who do you want to be like?" Inspiration teaches moves, not identities, and the question invites a copy.

---

## 6. Platforms and time

**Trying to learn:** where they post now and how often; where they want to post; real hours a week, split by kind of work; who helps; the tools they already use; hand posting or posting by machine.

**Open with:** "Where do you post today, and how often, honestly?" Quote back anything already in `content/setup/preferences.md` instead of asking it.

**Then, if it is still open:**

- "Which of those is working, by your own read? Any number you can see in your analytics is welcome, and a gut read is fine too."
- "Where do you want to be in 6 months that you're not now?"
- "In a real week, not a good one, how many hours can content get? How much of that is recording, editing, writing and posting?"
- "Who helps you? An editor, an assistant, a friend, nobody?"
- "What do you already use to record, edit and schedule?"
- "Do you want to press post yourself, or would you like posting done for you once you've approved each piece?"

**When the answer is generic** ("as much as it takes"): "What did last week actually look like?"

**Never ask:** "How many followers do you have?" If a number matters, it is the one they named in objectives.

---

## Closing lines that work

- "That's what I needed for this part. Next time we'll do [next section]. Say 'pick up my profile' and I'll start where we stopped."
- At the very end: "Here's the 1-page version. Is this you?"
