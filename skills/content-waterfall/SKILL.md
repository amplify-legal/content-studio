# Content Waterfall

**Load before any step:** `content/PRINCIPLES.md` (the constitution: it wins over every step
here), then `content/taste/README.md` and whatever it points at for the surface in hand, the
relevant `content/frameworks/*.md`, and `content/live.md` for what is in motion right now.
Taste is data and this skill is the engine: never restate a taste rule here, read it from the
file.

One recording becomes a week of output across every surface, governed by the creator's taste.
This is an engine, not a door. The `content` skill owns the interview posture and routes here;
arriving directly is fine when you already know which engine you want, but the posture still
applies.

---

## The posture, which governs every engine below

If a stage produces something the creator puts their name on (a promise, a spine, the
packaging, which clips become shorts, the angle of a post), **run the interview first**, per the
content skill's interview stages. Generate without asking only when they said "just draft it"
or "no questions", or when re-running a stage they already shaped.

This exists because a content system once produced an entire brief for an expensive video
without asking a single question, and the creator never opened it.

**The test for any run:** did it produce something that did not exist before they answered? A
sharper promise, a spine in their words, a framing they had not said out loud. 3 lookups handed
back is a failure, however well formatted.

---

## The iron rules

- **The machine structures what they said. It never writes in their place.** A derivative is a
  re-cut of their material, not a new argument in their voice.
- **Drafts only.** Every piece passes its person before it stages. Nothing sends or posts itself.
- **Only language leaves the source.** A client's or a customer's facts and figures stay
  private. A specific story needs their written permission.
- **Every number gets verified before it ships.** `content/taste/stories.md` is the only place a
  figure comes from. Anything else is flagged, not written.
- **Clients and customers are described by shape, never named**, unless they have said in
  writing that they can be.

---

## Engine 0. seed: any time they have a thought

The entry point the whole system exists for. Ideas arrive mid-thought, in a dictated dump, in a
stray sentence on a call. Whatever the channel, they land here.

**Order matters: capture first, riff second.** Never make them wait for the capture while the
machine thinks.

1. **Capture, verbatim.** Append to `content/seeds.md`, their exact words, transcription warts
   included. Never tidy or summarise a dictated transcript: their phrasing is what the taste
   model learns from.
2. **Riff, using the interview protocol** (`content/taste/interview/protocol.md`, in its layer
   order, never locking a layer before the one above it):
   - Read the map back: "Here's what I think you mean, and who it's for. Does that match?"
   - Calibrate the promise early: 3 or 4 honest framings, and ask which claim is true. In the
     first or second exchange, never at the end.
   - Pull the spine in their words: "What are the pieces, in the order you'd teach them?" Never
     propose the spine first.
   - Retrieval in service of their answer. One strong piece of evidence usually beats 5. The
     closest thing a real member of their audience said on a call beats any outlier video.
   - Stop when they signal the end. Restate the spine and write the riff into the seed record.
3. Ask a clarifying question wherever the transcript is fuzzy or the thought is half-formed.
4. **Capture only** when they say so ("just capture this", "park it"). Then step 2 does not run.

**Inspiration is a seed too, but it lands somewhere else.** Sort on arrival, without asking:

| What arrived | Where it lands | What runs |
|---|---|---|
| A video idea, a topic, a thought | `content/seeds.md`, verbatim | the riff above |
| A link, a screenshot, a creator, "I like this thumbnail" | `content/taste/packaging/inbox.md`, verbatim, with `by:` who sent it and their words on why | the packaging teardown |
| Both in one dump | both, split by sentence, nothing lost | both |

**The packaging teardown, the same shape every time** so the corpus stays comparable: actually
look at the thumbnail (download it), the title, the first 90 seconds of transcript, then how the
thumbnail, title and intro tell one story, then the mechanics stated as rules rather than vibes,
then one honest paragraph on what transfers to their audience and what does not. Then say back,
in one paragraph, what the new entry has in common with what is already in the corpus, so they
see their taste accumulating rather than a file growing.

When 2 people feed the same system, the creator's taste is the bar and the other person's is
input; a disagreement is a conversation, not a rule.

## Engine 1. mine: the weekly sweep

**Pure retrieval, no interview needed.** Sweep, tag, and hand the result to the content door,
which decides with the creator what to do about it.

Sweep the sources they have for audience language, questions, objections and the moments where
money or time is named: call transcripts, workshop recordings, the newsletters and creators in
their lane, internal working calls. Write tagged entries into `content/signal.md`, each with a
`status:` line (`fresh`, `shortlisted`, `briefed`, `dead`).

- **Open every run with last week's conversion.** "37 entries, 0 briefed, 12 now 14 days old."
  2 runs in a row with zero conversion means the run proposes mining less often rather than
  mining again.
- **Keep a watermark.** Record the last source item mined at the top of `signal.md` and mine
  only past it. "Since the last run" needs something to read.
- **Dedupe before reading.** The same recording is often stored twice under near-identical
  titles. Group by date plus title stem, keep the better copy, and report the distinct count.
- **Report the source split.** How many items came from where, and how many sources were
  actually your audience. A week with no audience contact should be visible, not hidden behind
  a healthy entry count.
- **Print what you skipped, with the reason.** A silent miss is invisible and compounds.
- **Resolve every attendee before mining a call.** A calendar title that looks like a sales call
  is often a peer, a vendor or a guest. Check who they are first.
- **Carry the standing question: which question came up on a call this week?** Sales and client
  calls are the best content feedback channel there is, and the transcripts already answer it.

The creator's own seeds are reviewed first, the mined signal second. A clickable board works
better than a document for shortlisting dozens of entries in one sitting.

## Engine 2. brief: before recording

**Interview first, always** (unless a skip rule applies). This is the highest-judgment stage in
the system. Run the content skill's brief interview: the map read back, the promise, the spine
in their order, what to leave out (and what to keep that a shorter version would drop), the
packaging candidates, the call to action and its slot, the derivative map with the beat in each
spine piece expected to become a short, and the key lines co-created with them.

Then write the document **as the record of that conversation**, from the template in
`.claude/skills/script-writer/references/brief.md` (Part 2), at
`content/briefs/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md`:

1. Write the interview state in the header: full (date), skipped ("just draft it"), or re-run.
2. Write down what they said, in their words. This is a record, not a proposal.
3. Key lines are co-created, never planted by the machine, and stay in their words.
4. Do the retrieval without asking: timestamps checked against the raw, sources, the number
   check.
5. Anything they did not settle stays visibly `OPEN:` with the question. An honest, unfinished
   brief beats a complete one that invented their opinion.

After recording, fill in the timestamps of the key lines that landed and the vision handoff
transcript.

## Engine 3. waterfall: off a finished recording

### Step 1. Watch the whole recording

Not the transcript. The recording. The `watch` skill gives you frames and words together.

**Clips are planned from the brief before the shoot and picked from the tape after watching
it.** A clip list assembled from a transcript alone reliably picks moments that read well and
play badly, because the transcript cannot see that they trailed off, looked away, or said it
better 40 seconds later.

Come out of this step with the real moments, timestamped, a sentence each on why that one.

### Step 2. Ask for the picks, with the whole map

Read the derivative map from the brief back to them, then **offer the whole tape map as
pickable candidates**, not a machine shortlist of 4. Anything deliberately left off the list
(wrong audience, weak delivery) gets named out loud with the reason, so they can pull it back in
one word.

Then confirm the map before anything is written:

| Surface | How many | The angle | Source moment |
|---|---|---|---|

Their answer to "what are you actually trying to get out of this" (in the taste README) decides
its shape. A creator optimising for clients gets a different map from one optimising for reach.

### Step 3. The chains

Run only the ones the map says. Each chain loads the transcript, the surface's framework, and
5 to 7 of their own approved references for that surface. **Never draft one surface from
another surface's output**: every chain goes back to the recording.

| Chain | Framework | Output | Who passes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shorts plan | `content/frameworks/short.md` | The picked timestamps plus beat notes, handed to `shorts-editor` one at a time | The creator picked the clips |
| Written posts | `content/frameworks/post-frameworks.md` | Drafts from `post-writer`, mined from the tape, never from the script | The creator approves |
| Carousel | `content/frameworks/carousel.md` | Slide-by-slide copy, one idea per slide, the last slide lands the point | The creator approves the cover |
| Community or newsletter post | `content/frameworks/youtube-pillar.md` | The same argument at the length that surface rewards | Whoever posts it |
| Send artifact | `content/frameworks/send-artifact.md` | One page built with `design-dna` | Whoever sends it |

**Never auto-clip.** A person picks. If nobody has picked, stop and ask.

**Every cut goes through `cut-review` before it is put in front of the creator**, whatever
question is still open about it. The open question rides with the review; it never replaces it.

### Step 4. Never pitch inside the story

A story ends where the problem lands. The call to action lives somewhere else, at the placement
the brief recorded, not at a framework's default percentage. This is the most common thing a
waterfall gets wrong, because every surface politely invites a closing line.

### Step 5. Leave a record

Write what this recording produced and what is still outstanding into `content/live.md`, and one
line in `content/work-record.md`. Then one dated line in `content/taste/lessons.md` on what this
recording taught: which moment carried, which clip died, which surface was not worth the time.
That line is what makes the next map better.

## Engine 4. sendlist: who it should reach

**Ask the framing, do the retrieval.** Propose the named people a piece should go to, with the
reason for each, and ask whether the list is right before writing openers. "Thought of you" has
to be honest, and warmth matches how well they actually know the person. The list is always a
person's call, never a generated list sent blind. Drafts only.

Whoever engages back (a reply, a meeting, a mention) gets logged against that person the same
day, and their questions feed next week's mine.

---

## Engine 5. scoreboard: the weekly roll-up

Run it as a visual, clickable board on the weekly review call, not as a prose agenda:

1. Every number the machine can know, pre-filled: views, subscriber change, shorts shipped,
   seeds captured, pieces launched on time, and **days since the last long-form recording** and
   **days since the last publish**, both read from the live channel, never a status file.
2. The judgment cells left empty to fill on the call: conversations and bookings traced to
   content, the people who engaged back, and the one improvement for next week.
3. 3 standing questions: which opening kept attention, which point created comments, which
   question came up on a call.
4. The one improvement chosen on the call gets written into the skill or checklist it concerns
   the same day, and next week opens with whether it held. The choice is the creator's; never
   pre-fill it.

The answers land back in `content/scoreboard.md` the same session so the file stays the record.

**Grading pieces fairly.** Compare each piece against the median of that platform and format
over the last 90 days (the median, never the mean), as a multiple. Grade after the numbers
settle (about 11 days), and re-base monthly. A piece at 2x or more gets a written guess at the
cause (topic, belief, delivery, packaging). 3 pieces of one format under 1x in a row is a signal
to stop that format.

## The workshop engine: all 3 halves

A live workshop or training is 3 things, not 1:

1. **A source.** The questions people ask in the room are the best audience language available,
   because nobody is being sold to. Mined by engine 1.
2. **A derivative surface.** The recording waterfalls like any long video: clips from the moments
   the room reacted, a recap post, and often a second video made from the workshop's core plus
   a new intro and outro that honestly delivers its own promise.
3. **A conversion event.** The people in the room are already leaning in. Give it its own tracked
   link, and log the attendees who engage.

Run all 3 whenever a workshop happens. Running only the first is the usual mistake.

## Publishing: after approval, never before

### Nothing posts without an explicit approval

Every piece that goes out carries who approved it. Recommended guards, whatever tool does the
posting:

- **Record the approval against the exact file.** Store a hash of the file at approval time; a
  changed file drops the approval and needs a new one.
- **One send per piece.** Read the post id from the first attempt before anything else. Retry
  only when no id came back. Never call a send twice "to confirm".
- **A missed slot is not posted late** past a sensible window (a few hours). Ask instead.
- **A placeholder never posts.** Run `scripts/copy_check.py` on every caption.
- **Read the surface back after every publish** and record the proof (the post id, the live
  URL). A composer going quiet is not proof, and generic errors have been seen on posts that
  actually went up, sometimes more than once.
- **One machine posts a given plan.** 2 computers running the same posting plan will post twice.

### Platform-native scheduling first

- **YouTube schedules natively.** Upload private, then set the scheduled publish time on the
  video. A publish date goes on only after the creator says go. Proof of an upload is the
  platform reporting it processed with a real duration, never a row in a listing.
- **Other platforms** schedule natively where they offer it, or post at the slot through your
  posting tool, or by hand. Use each platform's official API or its own scheduler, never a
  session cookie or an unofficial endpoint.
- **TikTok, as a generic note:** at the time of writing, TikTok's developer guidelines do not
  allow a posting API for a tool that uploads to accounts you or your team manage, and an
  unaudited app can only post privately. Plan on posting by hand from TikTok's own app or
  studio, or through a third-party scheduler that holds its own audited access. Check the
  current guidelines before you build anything.
- **Messages and connection requests to people are always sent by a person**, whatever tool
  posts the public content.

### Optional: a weekly skeleton with a nightly refresh

When posting gets to every day on several platforms, one structure that works:

1. **A weekly skeleton**, built once a week (Sunday works): one slot per platform per day, filled
   from the bench of finished, cut-reviewed pieces, a native caption per platform. The same
   video can go everywhere; each platform gets its own caption and hook.
2. **The creator approves the week** in one sitting. Nothing in it posts before that.
3. **A nightly refresh** reads the latest numbers and adjusts what is not yet posted: re-orders
   unlocked pieces, rewrites machine captions that a check failed, fills an empty slot from the
   bench, repairs a mechanical failure, moves a slot by a few hours.
4. **The night never touches the creator's own words or picks.** Mark those fields `locked` on
   each slot: their dictated captions, the pieces they chose for a day. The refresh may change
   anything else, and every change gets one line in a changes file with the number that argued
   for it. Anything the night fills or rewrites goes back to draft for approval, except a
   machine caption rewrite that passed every check, if the creator has agreed that approval
   covers the piece and the day rather than the machine's wording.
5. The night never approves, posts, deletes, or edits the skill and rules files. It proposes
   those changes for the morning.

The prompt for that nightly pass is `routines/posting-refresh.md` in this repo. It is optional;
a weekly plan posted by hand works fine without it.

## Retro, every run

After each engine runs for real: one honest paragraph (what worked, what dragged), and any
repeatable error becomes a rule in this file or the framework it concerns, capped at about 15
and pruned.

**A content run ends when the video has shipped**, not when the drafts exist. The end-of-run
review is done by a separate reviewer agent reading the whole conversation, never by the session
reviewing itself.

### Rules learned from real runs

- **Offer the whole tape map as candidates.** A run that put only 4 of 9 mapped moments in the
  pick question got "there have to be other candidates" and had to run again.
- **Resolve people before mining their calls.** 4 calls titled like sales calls turned out to be a
  peer, a demo, a negotiation and a podcast guest, and most of the reading budget went on them.
- **Trust the transcript you were handed over a health summary of it.** A transcript reported as
  having named speakers came back with anonymous labels on 2 separate pulls. Read the labels
  yourself before quoting, and mark attribution as inferred when they are anonymous.
- **Report the source split every mining run.** In one window, 1 call of 29 was with a real member
  of the audience and it produced every useful entry; the workshops produced none because the
  room was the wrong audience.
