# Your examples file

This file is a placeholder. What replaces it is the creator's own posts, annotated, and that
file is the thing that makes this skill sound like them rather than like a skill.

Nobody else's posts go in here. A writing skill carrying somebody else's examples produces a
competent imitation of that person, which is worse than a rough post in the creator's own
voice, because the rough one is at least theirs.

---

## What an entry holds

4 things, and the last 2 are the ones people skip.

1. **The post itself.** Copied exactly as it was published, line breaks and all. Not a cleaned
   version, not a summary.
2. **What shape it is.** Vulnerable opener, scene, named moment, encouragement, contrarian,
   long story with dialogue. A label, not a template.
3. **Why it worked, or why it did not.** Specific. "The opener named a body sensation in 6
   words" is useful. "Good hook" is not. When it failed, say what was wrong: the opener was
   context, the lesson was declared, the pitch arrived inside the story.
4. **The number it got.** Impressions, comments, profile visits, calls booked, whatever they
   track. A post with no number attached is an opinion about a post.

---

## The shape of an entry

The entry below is a structural placeholder. Every bracket is something you replace with a real
post of your own. Nothing in it is a real post, and nothing in it should be copied as wording.

```
## [A short name you will recognise the post by]

Posted: [date]. [impressions], [comments], [profile visits or conversations it started].
Shape: [vulnerable opener / scene / named moment / encouragement / contrarian / story with dialogue].
Star: [yes or no, is this a cadence reference]

> [Line 1 of the post, exactly as published.]
>
> [Line 2, exactly as published, with the same line breaks.]
>
> [The rest of it, exactly as published. Not cleaned up, not summarised.]

Why it worked (or did not):
- [What the opener did, specifically. For example: it named a time and a
  physical sensation in under 10 words, so the reader was inside a moment
  before they knew what the post was about.]
- [The line people quoted back, and why it landed.]
- [Where the media or the link sat relative to the story.]
- [The length, and whether any of it was explaining.]

What a model would have written instead: [the polished, generic version of
your best line. This gap is what the entry exists to record.]
```

### The same shape, filled with an invented example

Only so you can see the level of detail an entry needs. Delete it once your first real entry
is in.

```
## The missed-deadline post (INVENTED EXAMPLE, NOT A REAL POST)

Posted: [date]. 9,800 impressions, 31 comments, 2 conversations.
Shape: vulnerable opener.
Star: yes

> Tuesday, 11pm, and I had just realised I'd promised a client something
> I could not deliver by Friday.
>
> [...]

Why it worked:
- The opener is a day, a time and a mistake. No setup.
- The admission comes before any advice, so the advice reads as earned.
- 110 words. The last line is a plain verdict, not a lesson.

What a model would have written instead: "Deadlines can be challenging."
```

## Marking the good ones

Put a star on the posts that are the strongest cadence references, and load 5 to 7 of those per
draft rather than the whole file. Keep the weak ones too, unstarred, because a post that missed
is the clearest evidence of what to avoid and it stops the same mistake arriving twice.

---

## How it grows

Every approved post gets added in the same session it is approved, with its number filled in
once the number exists. Every rejected draft gets one line saying what was wrong.

Start with 5 or 6. That is enough for the skill to have something to measure against. It gets
sharper at 20, and it is genuinely theirs at 50.

`content/reference/taste-system.md` explains how a taste file like this gets captured without
turning into a chore, and the `taste-builder` skill runs that process and keeps the file current.
