# Carousels: what I rejected and what a great one is

> Taste is data. This file starts with one rejection from a working channel, written as a starting default, and grows from your own rounds. A rule enters only from your words, dated.

## The rejection that started this file (2026-11-04, a working channel)

The first carousel the machine built was a video frame as the whole background of every slide, a dark see-through box on top, a serif headline and 50 to 80 words of body per slide, and a source line under each. The creator rejected it as a pile of screenshots with far too much text, and asked for the machine to go and learn what a great carousel is first, then bring 5 different designs.

What that version was, so nobody rebuilds it: 9 screenshots with captions.

## Starting defaults, change them

- **A carousel is designed, not captioned.** A frame from a video is a photo on a slide, never the design.
- **1 idea per slide, in very few words, at a size that reads on a phone in a feed.** Check every slide at the size it is seen.
- **Learn from carousels that actually performed before building the first one.** Pull 10 or more real ones from your niche and next to it, and write down what each slide carries, the word count, the type size, how the first slide hooks and how the last one closes. Then build.
- **Bring 5 designs that differ on a real axis** (type-led, photo-led, a list, a story in steps, a single big number per slide), side by side, labelled, in 1 view. You pick; the machine never picks.
- **The facts on a carousel are sourced like any other claim**, and the words go through your voice check. A rejected design keeps its checked facts and captions; only the design is redone.
- **Each platform's own size**: 4:5 (1080x1350) for feeds that take it, 9:16 (1080x1920) where the platform shows a full screen, and a PDF where the platform takes a document post.
