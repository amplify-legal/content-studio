# Short

> Read `README.md` for the section order and how a sheet changes. The format family, the safe frame and the caption look live in `content/taste/shorts-formats.md`; this sheet says what a short is for and how it should feel. Every line below is a **starting default** with its reason; lines marked "a lesson from a real cut" came from review rounds on a working channel.

## 1. What it is for

Teach something and deliver a lot of value in under 60 seconds. A useful framing: what can you teach in 60 seconds or less? It is about the learner, not about you, and it opens and wraps up inside the minute. A short is a made thing, never a raw cut plus a 9:16 crop (`content/frameworks/short.md`, the bar; `content/PRINCIPLES.md`, "Transformation is the bar"). The hook stands alone: the first seconds complete a problem-to-promise arc without the rest of the video. And the short leads somewhere: to the long form, the consult or the community.

## 2. The frame you set

- **The same person as the long form, closer**: full-frame speaker, face framed and never cornered, the whole head with room above it, eyes around 40% of the height (`shorts-formats.md`).
- **The face sits high**; the mouth and chin never enter the bottom band, and the freed bottom gets a faded extension of the same video, never a black bar.
- A lesson from a real review round: faces about 50% bigger than a first pass put them, the background cropped off the sides. Later: the face box spans the full width, chin, neck and collar inside, nobody a small circle.

## 3. The opening

- **The first frame is the packaged promise** (a designed card in your brand kit), not a cold talking head (`frameworks/short.md`; `content/taste/clip-bar.md`).
- **Captions readable in the small autoplay for the first 5 to 10 seconds.**
- For conversation and tutorial shorts, lessons from real review rounds:
  - The first 4 seconds carry the aha moment or the question. Render both hook versions and compare them; do not argue it on paper.
  - A full-screen title card held for 3 to 4 seconds felt awkward and marketing-like, and was dropped.
  - A line strong enough can go first even if it gives away the punchline before the setup: the aha line, then the question, then the full answer, the guest's introduction at the end.
  - A hook title names who the guest is when the viewer would not know.

## 4. Pacing and length

- **Under 60 seconds unless the moment really deserves more, the arc of the story kept whole, snappy.**
- **Every visual change carries information**; the cards hold the visual argument, the captions hold the words (`frameworks/short.md`). Music ducks under voice; sound effects only on real transitions.
- A third party on the tape who is not seen, heard or named needs no consent; where their speech makes the short better, keep it and change the pitch of their voice only (`cut-review`, standing rule 3).
- Lessons from real review rounds:
  - Speech at about 1.1x to 1.25x.
  - A card is on screen only while it animates with purpose; a card that sits still too long reads as static.
  - A real transition on every scene change.
  - Keep the whole bit. A short that cuts the speaker halfway through the joke has cut the best part.
  - One clear purpose per short. A short that is all over the place is recut, not polished.
  - Context first: a short that drops the viewer into the middle of a conversation loses them. A few seconds of who the guest is, and the surprising fact about them, buys the rest.
  - Never the same clip twice in a row: an opening line that plays again inside the answer reads as a mistake (more from that round in `shorts-formats.md`, "Lessons from a fifth review round").
  - A candidate, not a rule yet (heard twice on review calls): a follow-up short made because an earlier short took off gives the audience what its comments ask for, usually the why behind the first one.

## 5. Packaging

- **Captions** (default look): white, bold, sentence case, soft drop shadow, no box, centred, 1 or 2 lines, the current word may lift or take one accent colour. Size floor checked at phone size: a 52px caption was "way too small" on a real phone, and about 78px was the floor that held; measure the capital height on the burned frame, not the font size in the config (`shorts-formats.md`; `shorts-editor`'s `check_caption_size.py`).
- **One frame for all platforms**, no per-platform variants.
- **Titles** come from the platform's proven shorts-title patterns; candidates go on the board with a box for the creator's own.
- Lessons from real review rounds:
  - The credit is larger text at the bottom in the last 5 to 7 seconds, rather than a chip running the whole time. Beside a guest's name the credit is the guest's own company, never yours.
  - A caption over a light ground sits in a box or turns dark.
  - The platform-native look is the default; heavy motion graphics on about 2 shorts in 10, and only with a purpose. Over-used, they read as AI slop.
  - Nothing on a face. A title leaves after about 28 seconds to make room above the head.
  - Small decorative text that takes space without adding value goes. Use the top and bottom space for something, or tighten it.

## 6. The CTA

- **The short pulls toward the long form, the consult or the community.** Value, not views, is the metric.
- **The full video is linked in the comments of every post** of a short (`content/taste/comments-and-pinned-comment.md`).
- A default: caption emoji policy per surface lives in `content/taste/voice.md`.

## 7. The edit

- `content/taste/shorts-formats.md` (the safe frame: nothing that matters in the top 12% or the bottom 22%, the right side dead from halfway down, captions in their band), `content/frameworks/short.md`, `content/taste/clip-bar.md`. `shorts-editor`'s `check_safe_area.py` is a hard gate.
- **Clips are picked by a person, never auto-selected** (`frameworks/short.md`, the input contract).
- **A short goes on the posting plan only after the creator has seen it or `cut-review` has cleared it.**
- A lesson from a real review round: no sharp peaks in a voice, and noise and echo cleaned before the short is cut, not after.
