# Podcast

> Read `README.md` for the section order and how a sheet changes. The pipeline, the frame spec and the episode lessons live in `content/reference/podcast-edit.md` and `content/reference/podcast-edit-runbook.md`; hosting in `content/taste/podcast-hosting.md`. This sheet says what an episode is for and how it should feel. Every line below is a **starting default** with its reason; lines marked "a lesson from a real cut" came from episode reviews on a working channel.

## 1. What it is for

A guest's world through your lens. Every guest is also a possible customer, a referrer, a case study and a channel to their own audience (`content/frameworks/youtube-pillar.md`, guest episodes). The viewer leaves with the guest's one real lesson and a reason to trust you for the next step. On a recorded peer call the stack and the price may be described, never a live CTA date and never a real contact's name on screen (`must-dos.md` row 14).

## 2. The frame you set

- **The conversation comes first.** Prep goes into making the conversation valuable for the guest: good questions built on what you know about them and on your last conversation with them. What the audience gets is a bonus when something good can be given or pointed out. A lesson from a real recording (2026-11-04): a planning run asked the host for the director's audience answers an hour before a recording, and the host's answer was to spend that hour on better questions for the guest, because a great conversation is valuable to the audience on its own. On a podcast the director's audience questions are never a gate on the recording.
- **The host who has done the work the guest is describing**: curious, present, never interviewing from a list (`podcast-hosting.md`).
- **Anyone named, introduced or addressed in a sentence is on screen in it.** A lesson from a real cut: the host named the co-host while the cut showed the host alone, and it was a fix.
- **The frame follows the people, not a fixed grid.**

## 3. The opening

- **A trailer of micro-clips**, 1 to 5 seconds each, chained hard cuts, about 25 to 45 seconds (`podcast-edit.md`, the opener). Not a long cold open. The picks are hooks and punch lines the host names, plus a couple of funny or surprising moments; a laugh is a pickable moment.
- **The trailer ships standalone and is shown as its own file before the full render**, because it is the part most likely to be redone.
- A default under trial: right after the trailer, one sentence on what you do and who for.
- **Name plates on from the intro through the first minute, and back for the goodbye.**

## 4. Pacing and length

- **Defaults from `podcast-edit.md`**: joint-silence gaps of 1 second or more tighten to about 0.6; never a kept sliver under 1.2 seconds between 2 cuts (the machine-gun guard); fillers stay in the audio and drop from the captions; false starts cut on word boundaries.
- **Content trims prefer list-like litanies over stories.** A trim that breaks a sentence's weave does not happen for a few seconds' gain.
- **In the trailer every cut between 2 picked moments changes the frame**, so the host's moments never read as 1 long clip.
- **Every frame stretch is checked against 3 questions**: what is a viewer watching at this moment, what do we want them to take out of this section, and is that objective being achieved.
- A lesson from a real cut: a trailer runs about 40 to 60 seconds at most; "too long" was the most repeated note on trailers.

## 5. Packaging

- **A thumbnail round leads with edits of the real frames, faces unchanged**, beside 1 real-frame option with no model edit and 1 built from real photographs (`content/taste/thumbnails.md`). Never an AI picture of a host as the photo.
- **Each platform keeps its own register**: a calm cover made for one platform is not the YouTube thumbnail.
- **Before any round, pull the channel's live thumbnails and match the register.** A podcast series has a style; a rushed round that ignores it reads as rushed.
- **Captions** (default from `podcast-edit.md`): uppercase bold, the current word in your accent, no scale pop; they sit below the mouths, and the music sits under the voices.

## 6. The CTA

- **Ask every guest before the record** (`youtube-pillar.md`, ask every guest): what distribution they have, what your CTA into their audience is (one), and what CTA they want shown for themselves.
- **Every person the piece names or shows hears from you the day it goes live.**
- A default: a guest's shorts posted on your channel carry the guest as a collaborator where the platform allows it, and the invite is sent to them directly, because some platforms send the guest no notification.

## 7. The edit

- `content/reference/podcast-edit.md` in full (the opener, the cut, captions, audio, the pipeline lessons). **Episode edits run through `long-form-editor` and the episode build pipeline, never `shorts-editor`.**
- **Echo is fixed from the source tracks before the cut; the true peak is measured on the mp4 that ships.**
- **A pick made on a mockup, a description or a rule is not a pick on the tape.**
- **A derivative inherits the episode's audio chain.**
- **The creator sees the file before anything is uploaded**, without exception, save one the creator grants themselves when asked, and there is one review call before an episode launches (`podcast-edit-runbook.md`).
- A lesson from a real cut: music is always louder than you think. Set it low, then lower it again, and keep music out from under a laugh.
- A default: post episodes in the order they were recorded, oldest unposted first.
