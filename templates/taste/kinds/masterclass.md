# Masterclass

> Read `README.md` for the section order and how a sheet changes. A masterclass is a tutorial's rules at full length, plus the assembly rule; where this sheet is silent, `tutorial.md` applies. Every line below is a **starting default** with its reason; lines marked "a lesson from a real cut" came from review calls on a working channel.

## 1. What it is for

The definitive end-to-end resource: one video that covers the whole job for your audience, compressed. Default length band: 45 to 90 minutes, never multiple hours. Why: a working channel that once shipped a multi-hour masterclass moved to this band because the audience's time is valuable and the job is to compress as much value as possible into it. It carries a particular kind of trust: you give away the actual system you run, not a sanitised demo.

## 2. The frame you set

The practitioner walking a professional through the whole thing, with the hours behind it said once (`must-dos.md` row 9). The same voice as a tutorial (`content/taste/voice.md`).

## 3. The opening

- **The 4 beats** (`must-dos.md` section B), with the finished output first mattering most at this length, because the viewer is deciding whether to give you an hour. The retention line names what they will build with you later in the video (row 10). The hook is written in full (`content/taste/script-format.md`).
- **Show the 4 or 5 chapters clearly in the opening**, so the viewer knows the route before committing to it.
- A lesson from a real cut: a short-video hook is not a masterclass opening. The opening that held retention set up the screen, the pain, the outcome they want, who it is for in one sentence, the authority, what is coming, and the guarantee, then said once that their time is valuable and the video is compressed for it.
- A lesson from a real cut: a line that filters ("if you already have the perfect workflow for this, this one isn't for you") builds trust with the people who stay.
- A default: the first minute is the most important minute of the video. Review it more than any other.

## 4. Pacing and length

- **45 to 90 minutes, a hard band.**
- **Sections first, then assemble.** Shoot the sections as standalone videos first, then one block for the cold open, the bridges and the outro, then assemble. Why: a multi-day recording block for one long video is what creates the stress. The cost, named: an assembled piece can read as stitched, so the connective tissue is written and every join gets its transition (`editing-rules.md`).
- An option that came up in practice: rehearse the hard sections first as shorts or 5 to 7 minute videos, so the arguments are tried and tested before the masterclass is recorded.
- **Chapters from 0:00, a section progress bar** (row 23).
- A lesson from a real cut: "do the thing, then explain it". And when the creator says "let's get into it", the video gets into it.
- A lesson from a real cut: a speed-up needs a reason on screen ("we're speeding this up, here is what is happening"). A very large speed-up (20x) loses people; a light one (about 1.2x) on a read-aloud stretch, with a zoom and a highlight, holds them.
- A lesson from a real cut: be ruthless about the long stretches (a 90-second interview on screen does not need 4 minutes), or viewers never reach the good part. Show the first few questions and the last one, so continuity is not broken.

## 5. Packaging

- **As a tutorial, plus:** the thumbnail test carries at least 1 real frame of the creator with what is on screen behind them (`content/taste/thumbnails.md`). The thumbnails picked, the description and the chapters are retimed on the final export, never an earlier cut.
- A lesson from a real cut: on a specialised long video with low traffic, test 2 thumbnails rather than 5, so each variant gets enough views for the test to read.
- A lesson from a real cut: serious, blocky, readable fonts on the thumbnail, big letters, nothing small, and no year or label nobody recognises taking up space.

## 6. The CTA

- **One CTA** (`youtube-pillar.md`). The mid-video connect ask (row 12) and the close (row 16).
- **At this length every promise made on camera is checked against the description before publish** (row 19).
- A lesson from a real cut: a cut prepared for a CTA has to deliver the CTA. A cut that stops the video to deliver a message, and then the message says nothing, is worse than no cut.

## 7. The edit

- As a tutorial (`editing-rules.md`, `editing-checklist.md`). **The ready date is 4 days before launch**, and the edit schedule counts to it.
- A lesson from a real cut: **text never floats on screen.** On-screen text sits in a card; a title floating at the top of the frame reads as unfinished.
- A lesson from a real cut: **a recurring progress card** (the stages of the job) returns at every stage change with the current stage highlighted and big enough to read on a phone, because 15 minutes in the viewer has forgotten what stage 3 was. "Coming next" can preview the next stage the same way.
- A lesson from a real cut: when the creator reads text on screen, highlight what is being read, in time with the words. When the creator says "this is a key lesson", give it visual reinforcement or it is forgotten.
- A lesson from a real cut: a wall of text on screen gets the few things that matter pulled out as big text ("Key questions: 1, 2, 3"), so the viewer knows where to look.
- A lesson from a real cut: when the screen has empty space beside a narrow window, make the creator bigger. Many people watch long form on a phone.
- A lesson from a real cut: when a resource is on screen, a short label at the top ("Free resource, no email required") and a pointer to the link below, each arriving about half a second apart, then gone before the resource leaves.
- A default: one accent colour per tool or brand, the same exact value every time it appears.
- **How a video is judged:** on click-through rate, retention and engaged views, read after about 10 days, never on day-1 views.
