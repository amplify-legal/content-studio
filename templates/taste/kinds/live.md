# Live

> Read `README.md` for the section order and how a sheet changes. The cut pipeline and its lessons live in `content/reference/workshop-edit.md` (and the worked pipeline in the pack's `examples/workshop-edit/`); this sheet says what a live is for and how it should feel. Every line below is a **starting default** with its reason; lines marked "a lesson from a real live" came from debriefs on a working channel.

## 1. What it is for

A conversation in front of a room, usually with a co-host, where a few topics are made tangible with specifics from your own experience. Why: saying "you can use AI to draft documents" makes nothing real for the people listening; an example from your own work does, and that is what the conversation is for. **Conversation over presentation.** The questions people ask in the room are some of the best buyer language you will get; mine them (`content-waterfall`).

## 2. The frame you set

- **2 hosts in a conversation, with more energy than a video call.** A lesson from a real live: a live room felt very different from a call, and needed even more energy.
- **Repetition is allowed for late joiners**, with a recap line ("if you're just joining us, this is where we are in the conversation").
- **Circuit learning**: "we've talked about 1, 2 and 3; now let's dive into 4".
- **Intros happen.** One person runs every slide, so the other never waits on a click.

## 3. The opening

- **The first 5 minutes are dead air to plan for**, while people arrive: a roadmap graphic on screen, walked in 30 seconds, not 90. A lesson from a real live: the roadmap was a keeper; walking it slowly was not.
- **In the cut, a live still feels live**: natural 1 to 3 second silences stay, no quick cuts; cut only tangents, distractions, mistakes and dead air (`content/taste/editing-rules.md`).

## 4. Pacing and length

- **Fewer topics, each made tangible.** A lesson from a real live: one topic made tangible ran 7 minutes on its own, and 3 topics filled 15. Plan the count from that, not from the agenda.
- **Quick value every 5 minutes**, so someone can join at any point and still leave with something.
- **The cut condenses a 45 to 60 minute recording to under 15** (`workshop-edit.md`, the sub-15 cut). The first 50 seconds of a cut need a segue between the story and the framework.
- **Shorts off a live: under 60 seconds, the arc kept** (`short.md`).

## 5. Packaging

- **The cover for the live platform is its own register** (calm, your brand lockup), never the YouTube thumbnail, and the YouTube thumbnail follows `thumbnails.md`.
- **2 real photos on one cover are matched on the skin, in colour**, never on a brightness number.
- **A host may ask for an AI edit of their own look** (styling, matching a second photo) and then it is built from real references of them; nobody's face is edited without their own word.
- **Attendees blurred, voices kept**, in any cut (`workshop-edit.md`).

## 6. The CTA

- **Follow up with everyone on the attendee list who actually came**, short (about 3 sentences), the recording offered.
- **Every page the live points people at is live before the live starts**, read back and returning 200 (`must-dos.md` row 19).
- **Prep is the biggest lever and finishes before the day before.** Name one host as the prep lead.

## 7. The edit

- `content/reference/workshop-edit.md` (the sub-15 composition, the traps, the per-pass lessons) and `examples/workshop-edit/` as the pipeline.
- **The full-resolution frame check** for attendee faces, name labels and private UI on every pulled frame (`must-dos.md` row 20; `cut-review`).
- **A workspace screen going on camera is rebuilt clean and recorded, never blurred in motion.** Why: blurring a moving recording has leaked faces and a private list between sampled frames.
