# Tutorial

> Read `README.md` for the section order and how a sheet changes. Every line below is a **starting default** with its reason. Lines marked "a lesson from a real cut" came from review calls on a working channel; keep them, change them or strike them.

## 1. What it is for

A viewer who is mid-journey leaves able to do one thing on their own data, bigger than they believed they could, and knowing where the walls are. Default bar: it answers a question a real buyer in your niche actually asked, moves a number they can see, and is something you would send to a named prospect with "thought of you" (`content/frameworks/youtube-pillar.md`, the 3 question gate). Authority comes from what you built, sold and delivered, never from summarising an article.

## 2. The frame you set

The person who built it, showing the inside and handing over the exact steps. A default shape for the line that sets it: "later in this video, you and I are going to build the thing you just saw, together, and I'll give you the exact commands". Warm and personal, story-led, earned authority, a laugh allowed, never a template voice (`content/taste/voice.md`). Customers described by shape, never named (`must-dos.md` row 15).

## 3. The opening

- **The 4 intro beats, in order** (`must-dos.md` section B): the finished output on screen before a word of setup, then the handoff into the body (row 7); a promise with a finish line (row 8); name and authority in one breath (row 9); the retention line before the first long stretch of setup (row 10).
- **The hook is a standalone 30 to 40 second unit, written out in full**, the idea first and the résumé after (`youtube-pillar.md`, Shape; `content/taste/script-format.md`).
- **The demo runs once off camera first** so the hook can open on a finished output (row 2). Open on camera, never on a text plate (`editing-rules.md`).
- **Straight in instead of the 4 beats** is a per-video choice the director records (`content/taste/director.md`, the intro-or-straight-in question).
- A lesson from a real cut: an opening with 3 segues before the content ("here's what I'm doing", "here's how I'll rank them", "stay to the end for the resource") reads as a video that never starts. One segue, then start.
- A lesson from a real cut: when the result is the exciting part (the agent answering, 2 outputs side by side), show the result first and the build after. It calls the viewer in more than watching the setup.

## 4. Pacing and length

- **8 to 14 minutes** (`youtube-pillar.md`, Shape). One idea per section, each built to stand alone as a short. Tutorials show where you are: numbered steps and clear progress markers.
- **Bullets guide the creator, the hook is word for word, the body is never a read script** (`script-format.md`).
- **Every section change gets a spoken or shown transition.** Why: a cut with no segue between sections is jarring.
- **A demo is the same input twice with 1 thing changed, and the screen shows the change** (row 13). **A long run starts before the take, so the camera never waits** (row 6).
- A lesson from a real cut: set up the stakes. A tutorial where nothing happens to the viewer if they do not follow along reads as boring, however good the steps are.
- A lesson from a real cut: about 3 minutes with nothing new on screen is where the next group of viewers leaves. Keep the section title on screen, add a progress bar, change the layout.
- A lesson from a real cut: a real demo is allowed to be slow, because the real thing is the proof; a viewer already 5 minutes in tolerates a longer stretch. Give them a reason on screen while it runs.
- A lesson from a real cut: when retention data says viewers are staying through a stretch, do not cut it because you are bored of it.
- A default: a stretch where the creator reads a prompt or a document aloud can run at about 1.2x to 1.25x, with the words highlighted as they are read.

## 5. Packaging

- **The package passes its 4 questions before the shoot is booked**: familiar format, open loop, who the viewer becomes, authority (row 1; `content/PRINCIPLES.md`).
- **Titles and framing address your audience directly** (`content/taste/thumbnails.md`). 3 titles are tested on every video, so the title question is multi-select and the creator picks the order.
- **Titles first, on text, at zero render cost.** The thumbnail is built on a real frame of the creator talking to the camera with what is on screen behind them, never proposed in words (`thumbnails.md`).
- A lesson from a real cut: a tutorial title says the what and the how ("How to set up X in 15 minutes") over a marketing line. Marketing titles read as an ad.
- A lesson from a real cut: check every thumbnail at the size it will be seen. No wasted white space, the words readable, and the face not covered by the duration badge in the bottom right.
- **The searched phrase leads the description**, your own links in their fixed order (row 22). **Chapters, and a section progress bar on every video** (row 23).

## 6. The CTA

- **One CTA per video, never stacked** (`youtube-pillar.md`, CTA discipline).
- **The mid-video connect ask about 30% in**, offering help with the thing they are about to get stuck on (row 12).
- **The close** (row 16). Every resource named out loud is shown on screen (row 11) and is a live URL in the description before publish (row 19). No live CTA date on camera (row 14).
- A lesson from a real cut: on a top-of-funnel tutorial, offer easy help instead of selling. "You'll try this, you'll probably hit a wall, and I'm here to help" is the value.

## 7. The edit

- `content/taste/editing-rules.md` (audio taming, a beat of tail on endings, opening on camera, a transition on every section change) and `content/taste/editing-checklist.md` (the mechanical pass `cut-review` runs).
- **Captions phrase-level with the current word in your accent colour, fillers dropped.**
- **Chapter times come from the cut's own transcript, never the raw tape.**
- **Fully edited, packaged and ready 4 days before launch** (`youtube-pillar.md`, the 2 gates).
- A lesson from a real cut: a comparison on screen is labelled plainly on each side ("Bad prompt" on the left, "Good prompt" on the right), and the one takeaway gets its own short text card ("You decide, not the AI").
- A lesson from a real cut: a file listing on screen is blurred by default. The editor cannot tell which names are real; blurring everything is cheaper than guessing.
- A lesson from a real cut: a visual cue used once (the screen going grey with a pause icon to mark a break in continuity) is used the same way every time after, or the viewer loses the meaning.
