# Editing checklist (the mechanical pass)

> One line per thing that has gone wrong at least once. A machine runs this item by item **before a person watches**, so review time goes to taste, not typos. It rules on nothing: it clears mechanics and hands the taste call to you. It applies on top of `editing-rules.md` and the piece's own brief.
>
> A checklist that starts at 5 real lines beats one that starts at 40 invented ones.

## Starting defaults, change them

1. **Minimal on-screen words.** Text carries keywords and structure, never sentences. If a card reads like a subtitle, cut words until it does not.
2. **Capitalisation is consistent.** One convention per video, held across every card, banner and title.
3. **Number formats match the audience.** Separators, currency and date order the way your viewers read them. Numerals as digits.
4. **Tutorials show where you are.** Numbered steps and progression markers, so a viewer scrubbing knows the shape.
5. **Every visual change is purposeful.** Motion for its own sake fails.
6. **Spoken options go on screen.** Whenever the speaker says "option A or option B" or "3 options", the options appear as a few words. Mechanical check: search the transcript for those phrases and confirm each has an on-screen counterpart.
7. **On-screen text for quoted audio is 2 or 3 word phrases**, never one-word flashes.
8. **No subscribe interruptions.** The ask lives on the end screen; the retention curve is the ask.
9. **Calls to action match the brief.** Count and placement as the brief says. <Your rule on how many asks a video gets.>
10. **Every resource named out loud is shown on screen**, and every on-camera promise has a live link in the description (see `must-dos.md`).
11. **Every frame that will be posted is checked at full resolution** for faces of people who did not agree to appear, name labels, customer names, and private UI behind the work (sidebars, conversation titles, file paths).
12. **The best part is in the cut, and reached fast.**
