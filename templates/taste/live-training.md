# Live training format: how a live session should run

> What the hosts have said out loud about running a live training, kept for the next live's prep to read. The lines below are **starting defaults** from one working pair of hosts, one planning call and one debrief's worth. Keep, change or strike each one.

## Starting defaults, change them

- **Conversation over presentation.** 5 minutes per topic item is a presentation, not a conversation; plan fewer items and leave room to talk.
- **A live needs more energy than a video call, and some repetition is part of it.** People join late and drop when they lack context.
- **The recap line for late joiners:** "If you're just joining us, this is where we are in the conversation."
- **Circuit learning:** "We've talked about 1, 2, 3. Now let's dive into 4."
- **Specifics from your own experience** for each item: the tool, the case, the number.
- **Plan the open as dead air.** The first minutes are people arriving; a roadmap goes on screen and one host keeps the thread.
- **A roadmap graphic is a keeper.** It anchors the conversation for the hosts as much as the viewers. Walk it in about 30 seconds, not 90; consider not showing every item up front, to keep some curiosity.
- **Fewer topics, each made tangible.** "AI can draft documents" makes nothing real; one worked example does. On a real live one topic ran 7 minutes and 3 topics ran 15.
- **Fast pacing on purpose.** These need to hold attention.
- **Prep is the biggest lever, and it is finished before the day before.** 1 or 2 planning meetings per live, one owner for the prep.
- **After the live, write to the people who actually came**, not everyone the platform counts as a viewer (a viewer count can mean the event page was opened). 3 sentences, with the recording offered.
