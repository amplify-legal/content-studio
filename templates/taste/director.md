# The director: what we ask before the camera turns on

> Taste is data, skills are engines. This file is the checklist; the **director** skill (`.claude/skills/director/SKILL.md`) is what asks it, and it writes the answers into the brief's frame section before the camera turns on.
>
> Where it came from: an editor on a setup call asked the machine to act like a video director. Before the creator sits down and goes into the flow, ask the team how the creator wants to come across, what the goal of the video is, what the one most important learning is, what the viewer is actually getting, why the viewer should care, whether there is a free resource, and whether this video needs an intro at all or can start extremely direct. Like a checklist, like a coach, every time. And understand, for each video, what we are trying to achieve in the viewer's mind.
>
> The 7 questions below are **starting defaults** from a working channel. Reword, add or drop any of them, and date the change.

## How it is asked

- **Only the delta.** The brief interview (`.claude/skills/content/references/interview-stages.md`) already asks the promise, the spine, what to leave out, the CTA and the titles. Before the director asks anything it reads the brief and the last 3 turns and drops every question already answered, quoting the answer back in 1 line. Re-asking is banned: being asked the same question twice is the most frustrating thing a creator reports about a planning session.
- **One batch, an open field on every question**, headers in plain words ("Come across as", "The one learning"), never a label. People riff in the open field; that is where the good answers come from.
- **The same phrasing as the person in the room.** The machine asks the questions the way your editor or director asks them on set, so the two sound the same.
- **The answers are the creator's words**, 1 sentence each, never a label the machine picked ("educational", "authoritative"). A label is a sign the question was not really answered.
- **The kind comes first.** Which kind of video this is (`content/taste/kinds/README.md`) decides the default opening, the pacing and the packaging. The director reads that sheet before it asks, so the questions carry the kind's defaults ("a tutorial usually opens on the finished output; straight in, or the 4 beats?").

## The 7 questions

| # | The question | Why | What a good answer looks like | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How do you want to come across?** | The viewer meets a person before an idea. A real example from a working channel: in one video the creator came across as a coach walking his team through it, a leader, and that one sentence shaped every edit decision after. | Who the creator is in this video, in a sentence they would say: "a coach walking my team through it", "a peer who already paid for the lesson", "the person who built it, showing you the inside". | The brief's frame section, line 1 |
| 2 | **What's your goal with the video?** | What we are trying to achieve, in the viewer's mind and for us. A video with no goal gets edited toward nothing. | One outcome: "they book a call", "they try the tool tonight", "they share it with their boss", "they subscribe for the series". Only 1; the CTA plan carries the mechanics (interview-stages, the CTA-and-slot step). | Frame, line 2 |
| 3 | **What's the most important thing or learning in this video?** | The one thing. If the viewer remembers one sentence a week later, which one. | The sentence itself, in the creator's words. It usually becomes the retention line (`must-dos.md` row 10) or a key line in the brief. | Frame, line 3; a key-lines candidate |
| 4 | **What are they getting in this video?** | What value is the viewer actually getting? This is the promise with a finish line (`must-dos.md` row 8). | What they walk away with, concrete: a setup that works on their own data, a prompt they can paste, a decision they can make on Monday, a number they did not have. | Frame, line 4; checked against the promise in the brief |
| 5 | **Why should they care?** | Who the viewer is and what it costs them not to watch. | The viewer named by role and the stake in money, hours or risk: "an operations lead paying for software seats nobody opens". | Frame, line 5 |
| 6 | **Is there a free resource?** | A resource said out loud is shown on screen (`must-dos.md` row 11) and is a live link in the description before publish (row 19). If you give it without an email gate, the default is to say so on screen. | Yes, with its name and where it lives, or no. "Maybe" is a no until it exists. | Frame, line 6; the CTA plan; the launch gate |
| 7 | **Intro, or straight in?** | Can this one start extremely direct, or does it need an intro? The 4 intro beats (`must-dos.md` section B: output first, a promise with a finish line, name and authority in one breath, the retention line) are the default; skipping them is a choice made per video, before the record, never discovered in the edit. | "The 4 beats", or "straight in, because <why>", and if straight in, where the name-and-authority beat goes instead (it is a rule, so it moves, it does not vanish). | Frame, line 7; the script's hook |

## The frame, said back in 3 lines

When the 7 answers are in, the director says the frame back and writes it under the answers:

1. **Who the creator is in this video** (answer 1) **talking to whom** (answer 5).
2. **What the viewer leaves with** (answers 3 and 4).
3. **What the cut has to protect**: the opening choice (answer 7), the one learning, the resource on screen. This line is what `cut-review` checks first.

The frame is written into the brief under `## The frame (director, asked before the record)` with the date and who asked (the session, or the person in the room). The vision handoff, filled right after recording, opens against it.

Why it is written before the take: an edit with no written frame gets judged against whatever the reviewer happens to like that day. A frame written before the camera turns on gives the edit, the cut review and the packaging one thing to serve.

## The must-dos that ride with it

The director does not copy the must-dos. It prints the rows of `content/taste/must-dos.md` sections A (before the camera turns on) and B (the intro beats) that bear on this video, each with its receipt, and asks nothing about them: they are rules. Section D (the "what did we miss" questions the moment the take ends) is named once, so whoever is in the room asks them while the set is still up.

## Somebody in the room

On a shoot where an editor or producer is present, they ask these by voice. The director's page mode prints the same questions and the kind's defaults as one page of big boxes so they can read from it, and the answers still land in the brief afterwards. Either way the brief carries the frame before the camera turns on.
