# Interview protocol: hierarchical discovery

> **Purpose.** A 15 to 20 minute conversational interview with you, the expert, that pulls your voice, framings, analogies and natural sequencing into `content/videos/<slug>/interview.md` **before** a script exists. The machine structures what you said. It never writes in your place.
>
> This is a method, not a question script. The layer order and the principles hold. The wording of each question bends to the video.
>
> **Worked examples.** Add 1 or 2 of your own real interviews to this folder once you have them (`interview/example-<slug>.md`), one per video shape (a metaphor walkthrough, a numbered list). The session reads the one closest to the brief's shape before it starts.

---

## Layer order

Never skip a layer. Never lock a layer before the one above it is locked.

1. **Goal, audience, value** (validate the map)
2. **Title, core promise, authority** (before drilling the spine)
3. **The spine**: the pieces, in your words
4. **Per-piece content and analogy**, verbatim, one piece at a time
5. **Hook corrections**: you react to the draft hook and edit it live
6. **Build sequence**: what to show first, in your order
7. **The mistake that sets up the middle**: the failure mode viewers hit
8. **Natural close**: what to do once the basic version works (stop when you signal the end)
9. **Calls to action**: placement, content, destination

---

## The 9 principles

### 1. Validate the map first

Read the brief's framing back and ask: *"Does that match how you'd actually teach this, or does something need to move, drop or get added before we go further?"* A one-word yes means move on. Anything else means restructure the brief before drilling any piece. Never interview on a wrong map.

### 2. Calibrate title, promise and authority in the first or second exchange

These shape the whole interview. If the spine is drilled against a wrong promise, the spine work is redone.

- **2a, title.** *"Does this match the honest claim of what a viewer walks away able to do?"* If not, offer 3 or 4 alternatives (principle 5) and lock the title before the hook.
- **2b, core promise.** Audit the hook's central claim. Does your actual argument deliver it? A promise that is not quite true gets softened now, not after the shoot.
- **2c, authority.** Authority lands in the hook without pitch energy. It is not always a number: volume of work, hours, time in the field, community size, a specific outcome described by shape, the absence of anyone else doing it. **Audit every number in the draft hook here.** First-pass numbers in a draft are wrong often, and an inflated number in a hook is a false claim in public. Check each against `content/taste/stories.md`.

### 3. Hierarchical discovery

Goal, audience, value, then spine, then analogies, then specifics. If you volunteer something out of order, take it, then loop back to the missing layer. A perfect analogy for a piece that is not in the spine is wasted work.

### 4. Push for your framing, not the writer's

Paraphrase back and ask "is that the right label?" The label that lands is the one you pick, not the one the writer reached for first.

### 5. Multi-option naming

When you say "you come up with a few", offer exactly 3 (4 for titles), spanning registers: literal ("the dashboard"), functional ("the visibility layer"), figurative ("the window into the system"). Pick by number. Never lobby. Never 2 (a forced binary), never 5 or more. The same mechanic serves 3 layers: piece names, the big-idea name, and the title.

### 6. Fact-check live

Brand names, words too complex for the audience, business details, tool details. When you flag one, lock the correction at once. Do not argue and do not defer to research: trust your domain knowledge.

### 7. Lock one choice at a time

Short confirmations ("Got it.", "That's the analogy."). Never stack questions. Push when an answer is generic ("what's the analogy that makes that click?"). Accept when it is specific.

### 8. Recognise natural endings

When you say something like "I thought that was the end of the video", accept it. The video ends where your teaching naturally ends. Over-extracting damages the working relationship.

### 9. Write as decisions lock, never at the end

Each locked decision goes into the matching section of `interview.md` between exchanges. Verbatim quotes for analogies and stories, named decisions for everything else. No filler, no questions, no raw chat. A revised decision is updated in place.

## Starting defaults from live hook sessions

Generic lessons from working on hooks out loud before a recording. Keep the ones that fit.

1. **Settle the words before coaching the delivery.** Nobody says a line with conviction before they know what the line is.
2. **A hook is a filter, not a net.** The right viewer stays, the wrong one leaves early, and that is the point.
3. **Phrasing you would never say becomes a standing rule in `voice.md`**, not a one-off edit.
4. **A safe, general sentence is a rejection.** If it could be said by anyone in your field, it is watered down.
5. **When the script blocks you, drop it and answer principles out loud.** Then write down what you said.
6. **The qualifier that defends the claim is usually where the fat is.**
7. **Cap the length at roughly the point it works, then add conviction rather than words.**
8. **The pass test is whether it sounds like you.**
9. **Pronouns are decisions.** "We" or "they", "I" or "you": who gets the credit changes the hook.

---

## Closing

*"That's what I needed. Handing off to the script now."* Then the script step reads the brief and `interview.md`.

## What good looks like

1 to 2 pages: the validated map, the pieces in your words, per-piece content and analogy verbatim, 3 to 5 hook corrections, a 5 to 10 step build sequence, the mistake story verbatim, the natural close, the call-to-action decisions. Longer than 2 pages means filler was transcribed. Shorter than 1 means the spine was under-extracted.
