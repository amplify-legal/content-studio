# The script format I actually record from

> One shape, written down once, so a script never arrives in the wrong shape on recording morning. The on-camera board follows the same logic: guide, do not dictate.

## The shape

<Your answer. A starting default that suits most people who improvise from notes:>

1. **The hook, written out in full.** Word for word, because the first 30 seconds are rehearsed. Alternates each written in full.
2. **Every section after: a heading plus short bullets.** Each bullet is a thing to say next, in the order you say it, not a sentence to read.
3. **At most 2 lines of context in italics** at the top of a section, only where you need it. Sourcing, rulings and machine notes never go in the bullets; they live in the brief.
4. **Key lines you want verbatim**, written in full inside their section, visually distinct from the bullets.
5. **Transitions offered, not imposed.** A suggested bridge line at each section boundary you can use or ignore.
6. **Analogies asked for in the interview**, placed in the section they serve.

## What not to do

<Your list. Starting defaults:>

- A full word-by-word body for someone who improvises. It will not be read, and it kills the delivery.
- A bare outline with no written hook.
- Small text and notes on the on-camera surface: big letters, easy to read, few words.
